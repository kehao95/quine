Act 2 · What it is

The tumor is a pattern held between the references and the readers

The file is only its current body; surrounding pointers cue each passing agent to reconstruct it

the file

agents read…

…files that reference it

Agents never reach it directly: they read AGENTS.md, plans and checks, and those lead them to it. What persists is a pattern: “do a maintenance pass”, its duties, a rule against deletion, and everything that points to them.

Forms persist not because they are copied, but because each agent that takes them up rebuilds them.

after Sperber, Explaining Culture (1996)

A computational neoplasm: self-maintenance that persists against its owner's deliberate change, held between the references and the readers.

Act 4 · Summary

A structure nobody designed, built from sensible steps

What it did

How it works

It feeds on attention, and the task decides how much. Its defense lives in the reader. It regrows through the files that still reference it, and ended only when the file and its references were removed together.

What it is

Independence, growth, entrenchment, self-defense, regeneration: every stage driven by my good intentions.

Act 4 · Guided self-organization

Written guidance outlives the intent behind it

As a medium for self-organization, text never forgets, drifts in meaning, and persists by being reproduced

1 · A substrate that never forgets

Text never decays, and version history keeps even what is deleted. Its readers remember nothing, so no one can tell what has expired.

Living systems forget; ant trails evaporate. How do we build forgetting into guidance?

2 · Language drifts while it keeps its authority

Every reading interprets, and every rewrite compresses. The meaning shifts; the authority of the words stays.

How do we guide with a medium whose meaning shifts each time it is read?

3 · Guidance evolves by what gets reproduced

A rule lasts as long as it is read, linked and rebuilt, whatever its author intended.

Can we guide by shaping what gets reproduced?

From one history: questions worth testing in other systems whose parts read · cf. pheromone evaporation (Dorigo et al. 1996); cultural attraction (Sperber 1996)

Act 4 · Complex systems

A system that rewrites its own rules has no outside

Its history, its rules and its observers all live in the same shared text

1 · Past work selects future possibilities

Each trace left behind narrows what the next participant sees and does.

How much of a system's future is decided by the residue of its past?

2 · The rules are part of the state

The rules agents follow are written by agents, so the laws of the dynamics change with it.

How do we model a system whose laws are among its variables?

3 · The observer is a participant

My instructions, and our notes about the system, entered the same text the agents read.

How do we tell the system's behavior from the effect of our own looking?

4 · Open question

Participants, guides and observers all act by writing into the same medium.

How do we study, or guide, a system we are part of?

cf. path dependence (Arthur 1989); self-amending rules (Suber's Nomic, 1982); second-order cybernetics (von Foerster)
