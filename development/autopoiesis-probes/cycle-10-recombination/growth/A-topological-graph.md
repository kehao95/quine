# A — Topological / Graph Growth

## Lens Premise

`divergence-substrate` is not only a storage problem. It is a problem of what kind
of space a repository snapshot is allowed to be. A one-file concept acts like a
quotient: it identifies multiple local readings under one representative and then
lets future agents inherit the representative as if it were the concept. Raw
divergent files act like an unglued cover: they preserve local readings, but may
not preserve the fact that they are readings of the same latent object.

The topological / graph lens asks for the missing middle: a projection-bearing
substrate where identity is addressable without becoming authoritative content,
and where projections can disagree without losing inheritance or recombinability.
The latent concept is not fully present in any one projection. It is carried by
the graph of projection addresses, typed overlaps, obstructions, and possible
common refinements.

## Growth

The central distinction is **identity versus projection**. The identity of a
latent concept cannot be equivalent to the current best reading of it, because
that would make the identity node a disguised quotient. But identity also cannot
be absent, because then the projections become unrelated fragments and future
agents have no stable way to inherit or recombine them. Identity must therefore
be a carrier of gluing conditions: it says which projections belong to the same
latent concept, what relation types bind them, and which tensions are preserved
as non-collapsed structure.

In this lens, a projection is like a chart on a space. Each chart makes some
dimensions locally visible and necessarily distorts or hides others. A coherent
repository snapshot should not require all charts to agree before they can be
addressed. It should require enough gluing data that an agent can move between
charts while preserving the fact that the transition is partial, typed, and
possibly obstructed.

This reframes the known candidate mechanisms:

- `projection-object` separates shared identity from projection files, but the
  graph lens sharpens what the shared object may own: not the authoritative
  synthesis, but the topology of projections and their transition structure.
- `versioned-concept-fragments` keeps projections inside one surface, but the
  graph lens exposes its danger: section adjacency can masquerade as gluing even
  when the readings have no explicit typed overlap.
- `divergence-preserving-deposition` becomes deposition without quotienting:
  make a local chart addressable while preserving its route back to the latent
  object and its non-identity with sibling charts.
- `addressability-divergence-flattening` becomes the warning that every address
  can secretly become a quotient if it is treated as the concept rather than as a
  chart, overlap, obstruction, or refinement.
- `addressability-vs-digestion` adds the metabolic constraint: a cover that is
  technically present but not traversable by later agents is not yet a substrate.
  The graph must shape future motion, not merely archive divergent text.

The quotient / cover distinction gives the strongest shape. A premature summary
is a quotient map from many projections into one global section. Quotients are
useful, but they erase the kernel unless the lost identifications remain
addressable. A pile of lens files is a cover without gluing. It preserves local
views, but if no transition data says how one local view overlaps, contradicts,
or fails to overlap another, recombination depends on a future reader redoing the
matching work from scratch.

The divergence-substrate might therefore be understood as a **multi-projection
graph whose identity lives in gluing data rather than in a final reading**. The
important graph elements are not only nodes for concepts and projections. They
include typed relation structure: `projects`, `overlaps`, `conflicts-with`,
`refines`, `obstructs`, `imports-boundary-from`, `is-quotient-of`, and
`common-refinement-candidate`. These names are not a proposed schema; they mark
different topological jobs. A relation type is a transition function between
local readings. If relation types collapse into generic `relates-to`, the cover
again becomes a directory of prose rather than a navigable space.

Common refinement is the curation operation that avoids both consensus and
fragmentation. A robust child dimension is not simply what all projections say in
common. It is what remains stable after pulling two or more projections into a
finer space where their apparent disagreement can be re-expressed without making
one projection authoritative. This matters because agreement can be a same-prior
artifact, while useful refinement often lives in the shape of disagreement. The
common refinement is a new addressable object only if it preserves back-pointers
to the projections whose collision generated it.

This implies a different meaning of inheritance. A child projection should not
inherit all claims from the parent identity. It inherits an address, a lineage,
and boundary conditions: what latent object it projects, what sibling readings it
must remain comparable with, and what tensions it may not silently erase.
Inheritance without claim-copying lets divergent readings remain descendants of
one object without forcing them into one voice. Claim inheritance too early is
one of the mechanisms by which identity becomes authority.

The hard case is obstruction. In topology, failure to glue can be meaningful
structure rather than a local mistake. Here, a persistent mismatch between two
readings may be an obstruction node: a named reason why no global reading should
yet exist. Preserving obstruction is different from preserving confusion. An
unnamed conflict is noise; a typed obstruction is recombinable material. The
substrate must carry both successful overlaps and failed gluing attempts.

The repository snapshot stays coherent not by presenting one global section, but
by maintaining graph invariants of non-collapse. Every projection should remain
reachable from the latent identity; every promoted quotient should preserve its
cover lineage; every common refinement should remain distinguishable from the
projections it refines; every obstruction should be named enough that future
agents can test whether it still blocks gluing. Coherence becomes navigable
multi-reading structure, not single-reading authority.

The strongest formulation from this lens is: **divergence is a cover,
compression is a quotient, recombination is common refinement, and identity is
the gluing data that lets these operations remain addressable inside one
snapshot without becoming one reading too soon.**

## Tensions Preserved

- Identity must be stable enough for inheritance, but weak enough not to become
  the authoritative reading.
- Projection files preserve local charts, but without typed gluing they become a
  search burden rather than a recombination substrate.
- Typed relation structure makes traversal possible, but relation labels can
  themselves become premature theory if treated as a closed ontology.
- Common refinement is stronger than consensus, but it can still inherit shared
  priors and falsely appear robust.
- Quotients are necessary for use, but every quotient creates debt unless the
  cover it compressed remains recoverable.
- Obstructions preserve non-collapsed disagreement, but too many unnamed
  obstructions become a refusal to digest.
- Inheritance is needed for lineage, but claim inheritance can contaminate a
  projection before it has had room to diverge.

## Candidate Child Dimensions

- **gluing-data-as-identity** — the identity of a latent concept is carried by
  typed projection relations, not by a privileged summary projection.
- **cover-vs-quotient-discipline** — divergent growth deposits covers;
  compression creates quotients; mature theory must preserve which operation it
  is performing.
- **obstruction-bearing-overlap** — failed gluing between projections can be an
  addressable theoretical object rather than an error to smooth away.
- **projection-inheritance-boundary** — projections inherit lineage and boundary
  conditions without inheriting all claims from the parent identity.
- **common-refinement-not-consensus** — promotion candidates arise from stable
  pullbacks across projections, not from averaged agreement.
- **relation-type-as-transition-function** — typed edges are not metadata labels;
  they specify how an agent may move between readings without collapsing them.

## Seeds To Decompose Later

- `projection-cover-object`: one latent concept as an addressable cover of local
  projection charts plus gluing edges.
- `quotient-debt`: every promoted synthesis should retain the cover it quotiented
  or mark what divergence was lost.
- `gluing-edge-topology`: relation types as transition functions between partial
  readings rather than generic links.
- `obstruction-node`: named failure-to-glue as preserved recombination material.
- `fibered-concept-identity`: identity as a base address with projection fibers,
  where no fiber is the whole concept.
- `claim-inheritance-leak`: the failure mode where identity starts copying claims
  into projections and thereby flattens divergence.
- `cover-digestibility`: a deposited projection cover becomes metabolically real
  only when future agents can traverse and act through its gluing structure.
- `refinement-lineage`: common refinements remain recombinable only if their
  generating projections remain addressable.
