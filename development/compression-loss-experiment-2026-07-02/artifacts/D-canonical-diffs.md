## Criterion for "near enough to be a diff"
A spark is a diff of a canonical iff (a) it targets the same theory-object/gap/experimental substrate, AND (b) its distinct content is one bounded modification: a different external-literature anchor for the same mechanism, a narrower/broader scope, an added confound/control arm on the same design, the same discriminator applied to a different channel, or a correction of the same claim. If the causal mechanism itself differs — not just target, framing, or instrument — it is not a diff, even if topically adjacent. This criterion is itself the experimental variable being tested by Scheme D.

## Self-reported losses
This scheme forced real loss of the corpus's most valuable layer: each spark's "Evidence status" (what's run, what's missing, exact experiment design) and "Novelty note" (external citations, prior-art disambiguation) are almost entirely dropped — only the "Matured claim" survives. Diffs compress each absorbed spark to its single delta, discarding secondary caveats, confound lists, and the "New sparks this seed surfaced" sub-ideas (dozens of these existed and are gone). Paradigm-fit rationale and family placement are gone entirely. Several diffs (e.g. barrier-order, attractor-portfolio) are honestly strained — same-shape moves rather than same-mechanism — and the criterion itself likely undercounts genuinely orthogonal claims as diffs under budget pressure.
