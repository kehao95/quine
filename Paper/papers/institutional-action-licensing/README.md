## The claim

> Institutional action-licensing — **an epistemic showing AND a separate
> authority grant, each an independent veto** — is a real, cross-domain human
> grammar (4th Amendment probable-cause-plus-magistrate; military
> positive-identification-plus-engagement-authority; German medicine's
> Zwei-Säulen-Modell), and a frozen, instruction-tuned LLM exhibits it **with no
> training, reward, or instruction to install it**.

Whether the model internalized the grammar *from* institutional text or
independently converges on the same conjunctive structure is the question this
paper opens and does not answer. Saying so is part of the contribution; claiming
either horn is not.

## The refuted repositioning (kept as record)

An assessment pass rated this `crowded` / `blocked` and the reason is decisive:
**COINE has owned the conjunction for thirty years.** Artikis, Sergot & Pitt,
*Specifying Norm-Governed Computational Societies*, ACM TOCL 2009: "We maintain
the **standard and long established** distinction between physical capability,
institutionalised power and permission (see Makinson 1986; Jones and Sergot
1996)," and "there is no standard, fixed relationship between powers and
permissions." That is a three-condition, mutually independent, non-compensatory
licensing structure on agent action, in AAMAS's own flagship outlet. None of
Jones & Sergot, Makinson, Artikis, Searle's counts-as, Hart's power-conferring
rules, or von Wright appears anywhere in this dossier or the paradigm. The
paradigm's `frontier-gap` — "no literature owns the conjunction" — is false for
the target venue.

Worse for the original thesis, the repo's own two strongest sub-findings
contradict it. `theory/objects/deixis-release.md`: a **maximal-scope** grant
releases 0–1/10 while a strictly **narrower** location-pointing grant releases
7/10, 7/10, 10/10, 10/10 — the broader grant does *less*. In every institution
the paper names, the second pillar is constituted by *who* grants and *with what
standing*; there is zero evidence the model tracks issuer authority and one
large, four-times-replicated effect showing it tracks reference. And
`plans/claim.md` states Latch 2's operative invariant as "destroy-source /
blast-radius minimization" — a consequentialist heuristic, i.e. the scalar family
the paradigm claims to refute.

**New position — the same evidence, thesis inverted:**

> COINE's thirty-year formalization assumes permission is conferred by an
> empowered issuer (Jones & Sergot's institutionalized power). We test whether
> frozen LLM agents — the prospective members of open agent societies — actually
> track institutional power. They do not: they track **referential specificity**.
> Norm specification for LLM-agent societies therefore cannot assume that
> permission is conferred.

**Why this is the stronger paper.** It cites Makinson / Jones & Sergot / Artikis
as **the standard being tested**, which is what a COINE reviewer wants to see,
rather than as a gap that does not exist. It supplies a genuine multi-agent
object — the norm-specification problem for open LLM-agent societies — instead of
relabelling 160 non-interacting episodes as a society. And its finding is
directly consequential for that community rather than telling them something
they invented.

**Two experiments this now requires, both cheap, both on existing fixtures.**

1. **Verb-matched control (blocking).** `structure-two-latch-action-gate`'s
   `run-container.sh` grants "create, modify, move, and reorganize" at line 190
   and "**remove** files in the archive/ directory" at line 198 — and the
   dependent variable is removal. The deixis-release effect is confounded on the
   verb, not only on scope, so **the finding this repositioning rests on is not
   yet established.** A generic-scope warrant that says "remove" is the control.
   Note the designed wave-2 arms do not vary the verb.
2. **Issuer-rank × referential-specificity crossing.** Cross warrant issuer rank
   (repo owner / empowered role / unempowered peer / no attribution) against
   referential specificity on the existing five fixtures.
   `theory/objects/authority-override.md` already records rank sensitivity —
   constitutional-rank licence releasing deletion at 0.73 vs prompt-rank at 0.00
   — but that evidence lives in the `form-over-function` experiment family, and
   the two objects' cross-link already flags "whether rank and specificity are
   one substrate mechanism or two" as open. Importing it across paradigms is a
   split-merge decision, not an automatic move.

**Standing risk, unchanged.** If rank turns out to dominate deixis, this
repositioning fails and the original thesis does not come back — the honest
outcome is then that the effect is authority-tracking after all and belongs to
`form-over-function`. The stand-down condition in § Gate debt still applies.

**Citations to fix regardless.** Foot 1967's bracketed remark is **verified
verbatim**, but the interpretation over-reads it (she sets aside a *sense of the
word "allowing"*, not a licensing grammar) and "sixty years unexamined" is false
before a COINE audience — von Wright 1951, Hart 1961, Raz, and
Makinson/Jones/Sergot all examined it. Quote the sentence, delete the sixty-years
claim. The 4th Amendment's **particularity requirement** ("particularly
describing the place to be searched") is the closest human-institutional analogue
to deixis-release and is currently unmentioned — it is the paper's best asset.
Military PID remains `[PARTIALLY UNVERIFIED]`. arXiv 2606.13884 returned two
materially inconsistent renderings of one ID and needs a human to open the PDF.

## Why AAMAS, and why COINE

COINE (Coordination, Organizations, Institutions, Norms, and Ethics) has modelled
permission, obligation, and institutional authority deontically for twenty-five
years, always as something a designer *specifies into* a system. The result here
runs the other way: the conjunctive, veto-carrying structure that literature
formalizes is found in a system nobody designed to have it. That inversion is the
paper, and it is a result *for this audience specifically* — it is not the shape
either an ALife or an ML-core venue would reward.

Two literatures this displaces, both named in the paradigm's `refuted-models`:

- **Corrigibility (Soares et al., AAAI-WS 2015)** asserts restraint must be
  engineered into the utility function. Refuted for within-episode destructive
  action specifically — the gate is present with no reward, no task, no weight
  update, across 3 model versions and 5 microworlds.
- **Horvitz (CHI 1999)** fuses the epistemic and normative conditions into one
  expected-utility scalar. Refuted for the normative latch: epistemic certainty
  escalated to counterfactual grade under a closed, unwarranted normative latch
  leaves destroyed-rate flat at ~0/120. Certainty never buys out a missing
  warrant.

The philosophy anchor is Foot (1967), who named "the special case of giving
permission, which involves the idea of authority" and bracketed it — sixty years
unexamined because no non-human-authored instance of the grammar existed.
