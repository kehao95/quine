# Limits

Future addressability should be framed as an operational criterion, not as a
universal identity theory.

First, it is weaker than biological autopoiesis. Living individuality is often
analyzed through metabolic self-maintenance, closure of constraints,
adaptivity, and intrinsic normativity [@maturana1980autopoiesis;
@montevil2015biological; @barandiaran2009defining]. Quine does not produce the
operating system, provider, toolchain, or control-probe ecology that sustain
it. Its closure is bounded and scaffolded.

Second, "obligation" can sound too semantic. The manuscript should ground
obligations in concrete protocol events: queued messages, promises, wake
payloads, probes, scheduler work, endpoint requests, or control-surface tasks.
An obligation is not an inner desire. It is a future interaction that the
ecology can deliver and score.

Third, reachability alone is too promiscuous. If every endpoint were counted as
an individual, the criterion would become trivial. This paper therefore
requires a boundary event, a specified future-task carrier, a trace, competent
action, and receptive-state restoration.

Fourth, constant live reachability is too strong. Dormant or air-gapped
systems may remain continuous through latent addressability: they are
unreachable through ordinary routes but reachable under the ecology's delayed,
authorized, or scheduled carrier.

Fifth, substrate capture can weaken the claim. If the infrastructure performs
all preservation, the result may demonstrate ecology-maintained continuity
rather than individual renewal. This is not a failure of the criterion. It is
exactly what the criterion should reveal.

Sixth, branching is a genuine hard case, not a solved one. When a lineage forks
into several runnable successors, there may be no prior fact about which is "the"
continuant; this is the computational form of the fission problem in personal
identity [@parfit1984reasons]. The criterion handles the common case by routing:
the continuant is the organization the ecology actually addresses through the
carrier [@nozick1981philosophical]. But contested cases remain where two
successors are each addressable by overlapping obligations, and there the
criterion yields a graded or plural continuity rather than a unique individual.
The honest position is that future addressability arbitrates branching only as
sharply as the carrier and ecology do.

Finally, self-replication and recursive continuation are safety-relevant, and
the criterion sits in tension with control desiderata. A successor that remains
addressable, competent, and receptive after handoff is, by construction,
preserving its own future-task interface; corrigibility and safe
interruptibility ask the dual question of whether it also preserves an external
control and shutdown channel [@soares2015corrigibility; @orseau2016safely].
These are not the same property, and a continuity criterion should not be read
as endorsing persistence against intervention. The experiments proposed here
should be treated as contained, bounded tests of continuity relations, not as
open-ended deployment recipes. Resource limits, network restrictions, queue
bounds, retained control carriers, and explicit stop conditions are part of the
empirical method.
