# Conclusion

The slogan is simple enough to sound obvious: what can still be reached by the
future and made to act is what continues. The contribution is not the slogan.
It is the separation it enforces.

Source identity, executable identity, process identity, memory identity,
endpoint identity, and behavioral similarity can all diverge. When they do,
the relevant question is not "which handle stayed the same?" but "through
which future carrier can obligations still reach a successor, and what
organization must exist for that successor to act?"

Quine turns that question into an experimental contrast. With self-source
available, raw streams and `ctl/wake` control surfaces make different
successor organizations viable. The reproduced self-object is not determined
by source availability alone. It is indexed by the future-task carrier.

The theory should therefore be claimed modestly and used strongly:

> Future addressability is an operational continuity criterion for
> obligation-bearing computational lineages.

Used this way, it gives artificial-life and agent-continuity research a sharper
instrument. It can distinguish copied artifacts from competent successors,
state preservation from organizational reconstruction, ordinary handoff from
post-boundary addressability, and bounded control-surface closure from stronger
autopoietic or autonomous claims.

It should also clarify the role of the Quine work. The Quine LBA is an
announcement, the main boundary paper is the evidence home, and this paper is
the generalization. Future addressability is therefore offered as an
operational criterion for obligation-bearing computational lineages, not as a
stronger claim that the current Quine traces already prove general identity,
full life, or generic self-replication.
