# Release Plan: Harness Engineering Is Architectural Amnesia

## Goal

Launch the essay as a strong single-shot statement, then expand only in response to actual early engagement.

This plan assumes a **new or low-follower account** where concentrated first-wave attention matters more than dumping a long thread immediately.

Default platform sequence:

1. **X first**
2. **LinkedIn the next day**

---

---

## Platform Sequence

### X on day one

Use X as the first live test surface.

Why:

- the essay is sharp enough to benefit from immediate argument-driven feedback
- the first wave of replies will reveal which angle actually travels
- X is better for testing which knife cut deepest before locking the more professional LinkedIn version

Look for what gets the strongest reaction:

- “stop rebuilding the OS in user space”
- “agent runtime belongs in the OS”
- the primitive mapping
- the Quine shell examples

### LinkedIn on day two

Use LinkedIn as the second surface, after one round of signal from X.

Why:

- you can refine the opening based on actual audience response rather than private instinct
- LinkedIn rewards a slightly smoother, more industry-framed version of the same thesis
- the platform's timing is less fragile than a single magical morning slot; weekday mid-morning and lunchtime windows remain strong, and some 2026 analyses even suggest later afternoon can work well

**Most stable default:** publish LinkedIn the next day around **11:30 AM ET**.

If desired, a more aggressive secondary experiment is **4:00–6:00 PM ET**.

---

## Core Strategy

Do **not** immediately fire a long thread.

Better sequence:

1. **Publish the root post first**
2. **Wait 10–30 minutes** and observe early replies, reposts, and what line people latch onto
3. **Add one self-reply** only if the post is getting traction or needs one layer of clarification
4. **Only then decide** whether to extend into a short 3–5 post thread

The point is simple: let the strongest statement earn concentration before you split attention.
