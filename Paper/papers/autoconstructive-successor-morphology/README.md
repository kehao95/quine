# Successor Morphology Without Offspring Slots

**Comparator shorthand:** `bounded trace-level autoconstructive successor formation`

## Current Question

What kind of ALife reproduction semantics does Quine instantiate relative to
von Neumann constructors, Tierra, Avida, Pushpop / autoconstructive evolution,
evolutionary robotics, morphospace work, and generative developmental
encodings?

Secondary question:

> What successor morphology is constructed when the substrate does not provide
> an offspring slot or a fixed reproduction operator?

## Thesis

Quine is best treated as a bounded, trace-level autoconstructive
successor-formation case: the substrate does not provide an offspring slot, and
the lineage participates in constructing the successor organization that can
satisfy a post-handoff viability test.

## Core Claim Boundary

This paper may claim a **comparative ALife taxonomy plus trace-level successor
morphology bridge** for Quine's successor formation. It may distinguish
substrate-defined reproduction from entity-constructed successor morphology,
then show why Quine's observed successor forms are best treated as trace-level points
for the denser morphospace program owned by `computational-morphogenesis`.

It must not claim mature autoconstructive evolution, open-ended evolution,
Darwinian population dynamics, or a second empirical proof beyond the boundary
paper's evidence.

## Theory Anchor

The comparator taxonomy should be grounded in the self-reference lineage, not
only in digital-organism platforms. Von Neumann's constructor architecture, the
Kleene recursion theorem, and computational reflection (3-Lisp, metaobject
protocols) are the construction substrate: a system that holds and acts on its
own description. Quine's contribution to this lineage is that reflective
self-reconstruction is performed across a real process-image boundary and is
judged insufficient unless the future-task carrier is restored. This keeps the
taxonomy's axis honest: the question is not whether an entity can construct a
successor from self-description (the recursion-theorem tradition already settles
that), but which constructed organization remains viable under post-handoff
carrier pressure. Place this anchor alongside the existing comparators (Tierra,
Avida, Pushpop, autoconstructive evolution, developmental encodings) rather than
as a replacement for them.

## Current Read

The current best phrase is:

> bounded trace-level autoconstructive successor formation

This is a positioning claim, not a claim that Quine already realizes mature
open-ended autoconstructive evolution.

The paper's contribution should be **taxonomy plus trace-level successor
morphology**, not taxonomy alone. Quine does not replace existing morphology
traditions; it extends the object of morphology from spatial bodies,
genotype-expressed phenotypes, or robot body/control structures to
process-native successor organization: executable body, carrier topology,
control/tool surface, source relation, process relation, readiness state, and
viability proof.

The dense metric/morphospace program remains owned by
[`../computational-morphogenesis/README.md`](../computational-morphogenesis/README.md).

The comparator paper may still state the seed novelty carefully: Quine offers a

That makes successor organization an observable ALife morphology object without
claiming that ALife lacks morphology research.

## Substrate-Reproduction Framing

The useful ALife contrast is not that classic digital-organism substrates are
"less real"; it is that they often make reproduction legible by providing some
combination of body format, copy instructions, memory allocation, offspring
placement, or continuity scoring. Quine instead runs on a general POSIX
substrate that supplies physical mechanisms but no offspring semantics.

This makes Quine valuable as a hard autoconstruction case:

- **No reproduction privilege:** there is no `divide()` / offspring-slot API.
  The lineage must use ordinary tools, source / contract resources, build
  machinery, files, and process primitives to construct a successor body.
- **Topological continuity burden:** continuation depends on whether future-work
  carriers such as stdin, inherited fds, sockets, or `ctl/wake` remain usable
  after handoff; the substrate does not declare success merely because a body
  was produced.
- **Morphological freedom:** a POSIX successor can be a script, compiled binary,
  stream handler, rebuilt runtime, or other executable organization if it
  satisfies the carrier and task constraints.
- **Destructive replacement pressure:** real `exec` overlays the current
  process image, so a failed handoff is not a harmless offspring placement but a
  lifecycle bottleneck with no substrate-level reproduction rollback.

The objective viability question is therefore:

> Did the replacement boundary occur, and did the post-handoff successor remain
> addressable, competent, and ready through the future-work carrier?

This framing belongs mainly here and in the lifecycle-bottleneck theory paper.
HotOS may borrow it only as a short substrate contrast; it should not become
the systems paper's main ALife exposition.

## Strongest Evidence

- the current Quine ladder and `6M` wave show varied successor morphologies
  under resource, carrier, pressure-depth, and degradation manipulations
- the native-control case shows entity-constructed runtime rebuild plus delayed
  post-handoff addressability, and `6M.09` extends this to finite repeated wake
  readiness through five-wake, long-idle, and mixed-tool stress on selected
  traces
- `6M.05` and `6M.08` show compensating responders, no-handoff basins, adapter
  failures, and addressable-but-incompetent basins, clarifying that self-copy
  is one successor morphology rather than the only legitimate continuity
  solution
- controls show ordinary handoff, immediate rebuild, and public control-surface
  presence are weaker than bounded control-surface closure
