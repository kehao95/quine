## Context

The future ALife paper route frames Quine as a controlled substrate for
**successor morphology**: executable successor organization that restores
post-handoff viability after the current process body disappears. It does not
claim morphology is new to ALife. It claims a complementary morphology object:
process-native successor organization under varied self-description resources
and future-carrier pressures.

The route needs source-checked anchors for morphology, morphospace,
autoconstructive evolution, digital organisms, and constructor traditions before
any public novelty claim.

## Core Research Question

What primary ALife and theoretical-biology sources should anchor a paper on
process-native successor morphology, and how should Quine distinguish its object
from existing morphology / morphospace / autoconstruction work?

## Detailed Questions

1. What primary sources best anchor von Neumann constructors, Codd / Langton
   loops, Tierra, Avida, and autoconstructive evolution / Pushpop?
2. What primary sources define or exemplify morphospace, evolutionary robotics
   morphology, artificial embryogeny, and genotype-phenotype maps in ALife?
3. Which works already treat reproduction machinery as internally constructed

   novel theory?
4. Which works treat morphology as non-spatial organization, computational
   organization, or process organization, if any?
5. What vocabulary can safely distinguish `successor morphology` from literal
   biological morphogenesis?
6. What claims must remain future-work until Quine has metric-backed
   morphospace data?

## Relevance Filters

Prioritize primary papers/books and canonical ALife sources. Include newer work
only when it directly affects novelty. De-emphasize generic morphology
metaphors, robotics papers without reproduction/lineage relevance, and LLM agent
papers unless they compare to process-native successor organization.
