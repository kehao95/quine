## Context

Quine is an LM-driven process lineage in which the substrate does not predefine
an offspring slot or reproduction instruction. Under different conditions it
constructs different successors: contract-level cleanroom successor, reduced
stream runtime, or rebuilt Quine runtime with delayed control-surface
addressability.

The theory says:

```text
self-description resource x task pressure -> successor morphology
```

We need a deeper ALife/computation literature pass on systems where organisms,
programs, agents, or constructors construct their own offspring strategies, and
where environmental or task pressure can change the morphology of the successor
rather than merely selecting among exact copies.

## Core Research Question

What ALife, digital evolution, universal-constructor, genetic programming,
self-modifying program, and artificial chemistry literatures treat reproduction
as internally constructed successor morphology rather than substrate-defined
copying?

## Detailed Questions

1. **Autoconstructive evolution:** What exactly do Spector, Pushpop, PushGP,
   autoconstructive evolution, and related systems show about entities
   constructing their own offspring? Do they allow environmental pressure to
   select different reproductive strategies or successor morphologies?
2. **Universal constructors and description tapes:** How do von Neumann-style
   self-reproducing automata separate constructor, copier, controller, and
   description? Do later universal-constructor or artificial-chemistry works
   relax fixed-copy assumptions?
3. **Tierra/Avida contrast:** How much do Tierra, Avida, and classical digital
   organisms predefine reproduction? Are there variants where reproduction
   strategy itself evolves or changes with pressure?
4. **Genotype-phenotype maps and developmental encodings:** In genetic
   programming, evolutionary developmental systems, L-systems, cellular
   automata, artificial chemistries, or evo-devo ALife, how do developmental
   resources and environmental conditions shape phenotype/successor form?
5. **Self-modifying and self-hosting computation:** Are there computational
   examples where a system rebuilds a reduced successor, a full successor, or a
   contract-derived successor depending on task pressure?
6. **Selection language:** What terminology distinguishes population-level
   Darwinian selection from operational viability testing in one retained
   trace? How should Quine talk about pressure without overstating selection?
7. **Closest near misses and novelty:** Which prior systems most closely match
   Quine's resource-pressure successor morphology, and what remains novel about
   using native process handoff, source absence, source visibility, and delayed
   control-surface addressability?

## Relevance Filters

Include works that help distinguish:

- substrate-defined copying versus organism-constructed reproduction
- exact clone versus variable successor morphology
- description tape/source versus constructor/runtime
- environmental pressure/task pressure shaping reproductive strategy
- population selection versus operational viability testing
- ALife near-misses to Quine's process-native handoff

Exclude or de-emphasize:

- ordinary evolutionary algorithms where an external algorithm performs all
  reproduction and mutation
- malware propagation unless it theorizes self-construction or successor forms
- generic LLM agents unless they connect to ALife reproduction semantics

## Manuscript Payoff

This should clarify whether Quine's observed ladder is best framed as a
process-native instance of autoconstructive successor formation, and which ALife
systems are the closest comparators for reviewer positioning.

## Red Lines

Do not claim Quine demonstrates open-ended evolution, population-level
selection, or mature autoconstructive evolution. The current evidence is
operational/trace-level and should remain bounded.
