## Context

Quine is an LM-driven Unix-process lineage studied as an artificial-life medium.
The experiments impose post-handoff future tasks: current incarnation alone is
insufficient, so the lineage must construct some successor mechanism. Observed
successor morphologies vary with resource and pressure: contract-level
cleanroom successor, reduced stream successor, and rebuilt runtime with native
control-surface closure.

The emerging theory is that self-reproduction is not primitive copying. It is a
special case of successor formation under a lifecycle bottleneck: mortality,
handoff, channel survival, and future-task pressure force an organization to
collapse, compress, or rebuild in whatever morphology remains viable.

## Core Research Question

Do biology, ALife, complex systems, autonomy, and philosophy of biology treat
reproduction as a solution to lifecycle bottlenecks, precariousness, or
organizational continuity constraints rather than as a primitive copying act?

## Detailed Questions

1. **Lifecycle bottlenecks and individuality:** How do biological theories use
   bottlenecks, germline/soma separation, unicellular stages, developmental
   cycles, and major transitions to explain individuality and reproduction?
2. **Precariousness and adaptivity:** In enactive/autonomy literature, is
   reproduction framed as one response to viability limits or precarious
   self-maintenance? How do authors distinguish self-maintenance, repair,
   regeneration, and reproduction?
3. **Organizational continuity through material turnover:** How do autopoiesis,
   closure of constraints, and process ontology treat identity as maintained
   through ongoing regeneration rather than sameness of matter?
4. **Morphological solution language:** Are there theories where reproduction
   is an adaptive morphology, life-cycle strategy, or developmental solution to
   ecological pressure rather than a universal primitive?
5. **Regeneration versus reproduction:** How do regeneration, fission,
   budding, fragmentation, spores, seeds, and dormancy blur the boundary
   between repair and reproduction? Which examples best match Quine's
   handoff/mortality structure?
6. **ALife and digital lineages:** Do ALife systems discuss reproduction as
   crossing a resource/execution bottleneck, continuity bottleneck, or
   lineage-preservation pressure? Are autoconstructive or open-ended systems
   better fits than Tierra/Avida?
7. **Critical boundaries:** What literature would object that externally
   imposed pressure and admissible self-copy make this only an engineered test,
   not reproduction proper? What wording preserves the useful ontological
   insight without overclaim?

## Relevance Filters

Include works that help distinguish:

- reproduction as copying versus reproduction as continuity through a bottleneck
- repair/regeneration/reproduction boundaries
- individual identity across lifecycle transitions
- viability pressure and precariousness
- morphology/life-cycle strategy as solution to ecological constraints
- endogenous versus imposed bottlenecks

Exclude or de-emphasize:

- popular accounts of reproduction that only describe sexual/asexual taxonomy
- AI self-replication risk reports unless they discuss lifecycle or identity
- generic resilience/failover papers without lineage or reproduction concepts

## Manuscript Payoff

This should help articulate what Quine reveals about self-reproduction itself:
copying may be a special morphological solution to preserving organization
across a lifecycle bottleneck, not the primitive phenomenon.

## Red Lines

Do not claim Quine has biological life cycles, endogenous metabolism, germline,
or full organismal individuality. The goal is analogy and ontology, not
biological equivalence.
