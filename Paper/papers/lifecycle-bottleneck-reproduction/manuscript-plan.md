# Manuscript Plan: Self-Reproduction as Lifecycle-Bottleneck Successor Formation

The dossier owns thesis, scope, and evidence posture. This plan owns only the
future argument order.

## Argument Plan

1. Start from the conceptual problem: copying is too narrow to define
   reproduction when organization must cross a lifecycle bottleneck.
2. Define lifecycle bottleneck, self-description material/degradation,
   future-carrier/capability demand, viability-depth, and successor morphology.
3. Develop the repair / regeneration / reproduction continuum.
4. Use Quine as an operational case study without making Quine carry the whole
   biological theory.
5. End with boundaries: externally imposed pressure, scaffolded successor
   mission, no full autopoiesis, no open-ended evolution.

## Section Skeleton

### 1. Problem: Copying Is Not The Primitive

- why body/source/artifact copying is too narrow
- why bottlenecks expose the continuity problem
- what an operational artificial system can clarify

### 2. Lifecycle Bottleneck And Successor Morphology

- lifecycle bottleneck definition
- successor morphology definition
- self-description material/degradation, future-carrier demand, and
  viability-depth
- carrier / capability overlap as the operational Quine analogue to material
  overlap: the post-bottleneck issue is not only what formal description was
  copied, but which future-obligation carrier can still reach and obligate the
  successor

### 3. Repair, Regeneration, Reproduction

- repair: local defect inside current incarnation
- regeneration: substantial loss with recoverable pattern / contract
- reproduction: current incarnation cannot cross bottleneck intact
- formal reconstruction: rebuilding a body or implementation without yet
  proving future-addressable continuity

### 4. Quine As Operational Case

- contract-level source-absent successor
- reduced source-visible stream successor
- rebuilt runtime under native-control pressure
- degraded-source compensation, addressable-but-incompetent basins, and
  repeated-wake readiness depth
- carrier/capability-overlap contrast from `6M.10`: source/body material,
  process replacement, carrier delivery, payload competence, and ready-state
  recovery can separate
- prompt-minimized boundary and remaining scaffold

### 5. Boundaries And Later Work

- externally imposed pressure
- no endogenous drive or population-level evolution
- relation to future addressability and computational morphogenesis

## Expanded Manuscript Shape

The paper should be a theory paper with Quine as an operational case study, not
a Quine result paper. A good draft will make the theory useful even if the
reader treats Quine as one bounded artificial example.

### Proposed Contribution Bullets

- a lifecycle-bottleneck account of self-reproduction;
- a repair / regeneration / reproduction continuum based on scale of threatened
  organization;
- a distinction between self-description resources: runtime contract,
  source-code, body, state, and ecological scaffold;
- a pressure/resource/depth account of successor morphology;
- a bounded Quine case study showing why copying is not the primitive.

### Literature Slots To Fill

- formal versus material reproduction;
- reproducer / progeneration / material overlap theories;
- developmental systems, niche construction, and ecological inheritance;
- repair, regeneration, fission, budding, dormancy, spores, and lifecycle
  bottlenecks;
- autopoiesis and precariousness as ceiling concepts, not claims about Quine.

## Future Experiment Design For This Paper

The theory paper needs experiments that separate bottleneck severity,
self-description resource, and viability pressure.

| Experiment idea | Question it answers | Expected manuscript role |
|-----------------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| bottleneck severity ladder | When does a problem remain repair, become regeneration, or require successor formation? | operationalizes continuum |
| resource ladder | How does morphology change with body available, runtime contract only, source visible, degraded source, or full toolchain? | self-description-resource axis |
| pressure ladder | How does morphology change from no future task, raw stream, structured stream, native control, multi-wake, and carrier-repair pressure? | viability-pressure axis |
| pressure/resource mismatch matrix | Which mismatches produce collapse, reduced successors, or failed closure? | main theory stress test |
| carrier/capability overlap ladder | When does formal reconstruction cross the bottleneck without restoring operational continuity? | imports material-overlap intuition without treating source as the continuity criterion |
| endogenous-trigger probe | Can Quine initiate successor formation without an explicit future task? | boundary to endogenous reproduction |
| scaffold-reduction ladder | Which parts of successor mission / runtime contract are still doing the reproduction work? | limits / non-claims |
| regeneration-versus-reproduction control | Can the same organization be restored inside current incarnation versus across `exec`? | distinguishes repair/regeneration/reproduction |

Design rule:

- every experiment should state the lifecycle bottleneck, what organization is
  threatened, what self-description resource is available, and what future
  viability pressure is imposed;
- report the successor morphology, not only pass/fail;
- treat externally imposed pressure as a boundary unless endogenous-trigger
  evidence is added.
