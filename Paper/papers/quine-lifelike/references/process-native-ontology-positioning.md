# Process-Native Ontology and Core Positioning

This note synthesizes external literature relevant to Quine's newer core positioning:

- process-native individuality as the upstream ontological commitment
- separable viability pressures as the ALIFE-facing methodological payoff
- bounded continuity / inheritance / maintenance as the empirical demonstration

It is a reference-side positioning note, not manuscript prose.

## Current Read

The most useful external framing for Quine's new center is **not** generic `LLM agents + ALIFE`.

It is the intersection of three literatures:

1. **process philosophy of biology**
2. **biological autonomy / organizational closure**
3. **agent-runtime architecture**

Taken together, they support a disciplined positioning:

- Quine's deepest distinction is that the individual is a **native process**
- that ontological commitment makes death, volatile memory loss, persistence, and detached execution **substrate facts**
- the current ALIFE paper should cash that out as **methodological tractability**, not as a grand metaphysical victory

## 1. Process Philosophy of Biology: Why `individual = process` is a legible move

### Strong support

`NicholsonDupre2018EverythingFlows` is the clearest high-level anchor.

Its core claim is that the living world is organized as an interdependent hierarchy of processes and that even paradigmatic biological individuals such as organisms are often better understood as processes than as static things.

Why this matters for Quine:

- it gives external language for saying that individuality need not be tied to material persistence
- it helps legitimize a lineage-centered view where identity is carried by organized continuity rather than by uninterrupted stuff

`Nicholson2018ReconceptualizingOrganism` sharpens this into a more usable slogan:

- activity is a necessary condition for existence
- persistence is grounded in continuous self-maintenance of form

That is particularly useful for Quine because it aligns with the substrate facts the paper already has:

- a process exists only while it runs
- persistence across death requires active reconstitution
- maintenance should be read in terms of ongoing preservation, not static storage

### Even more useful than a strong metaphysical claim: Pradeu's methodological reading

`Pradeu2018GenidentityBiologicalProcesses` is especially helpful for paper discipline.

Its key move is to argue that the main interest of adopting a process approach may be **epistemological rather than ontological**:

- processes and things can both be useful
- processes are prior when the scientific question is about identity-through-time
- what matters is that following processes changes what one tracks, explains, and experiments on

This is almost exactly the safe use-case for Quine in the current ALIFE paper.

Implication:

- do **not** write as if the paper had proven that all agent individuality is processual
- do write that Quine adopts a process-native ontology because it makes lineage, death, inheritance, and maintenance experimentally legible

## 1.5 Historical ALIFE precedents for process-native individuals

This new process-ontology research sharpens an important novelty boundary.

The strongest version of Quine's ontological claim is **not**:

- no one in ALIFE has ever treated digital individuals as processes

That would be too broad. Older distributed-object / digital-organism systems such as Fuchs's `DREME` and the `Eden` language already explored agents associated with Unix processes rather than only with simulation-side objects.

What remains unusual, and still defensible, is narrower:

- ALIFE rarely made the distinction between **framework-managed agent objects** and **native OS processes** a central ontological or methodological issue
- ALIFE almost never connected that distinction explicitly to contemporary biological process ontology
- Quine turns that process-native premise into an experimental method for studying death, persistence, and maintenance

Implication for the paper:

- do not claim Quine invented process-native digital individuals
- do claim that Quine makes the process/object distinction methodologically explicit and experimentally productive in a current soft-ALIFE / LLM setting

## 2. Biological Autonomy and Organizational Closure: Useful, but a higher bar

`MorenoMossio2015BiologicalAutonomy` and `Meincke2019AutopoiesisProcessView` are the most useful sources for understanding what stronger autonomy language would require.

The good news:

- both link living organization to self-maintenance, autonomy, and organizational closure
- `Meincke2019AutopoiesisProcessView` explicitly argues that autopoiesis and biological autonomy are best understood through a process ontology

Why this matters for Quine:

- it supports the intuition that detached maintenance is not just a cute engineering trick
- it gives a principled reason why maintenance should matter to individuation and persistence

But this literature also raises the bar sharply:

- autonomy is about self-production and self-maintenance as an organized regime
- closure is stronger than note-taking, checkpointing, or one-shot repair

Implication for the conference paper:

- use this literature to understand the **ceiling**, not to inflate the current claim
- detached maintenance can be framed as a bounded environmental maintenance organ
- full autopoiesis / organizational closure should remain downstream

## 2.5 Extended phenotype, extended organism, and environmental organs

The new background on extended phenotype and scaffolded cognition is useful, but it also sets a discipline rule for the paper's terminology.

`Dawkins1982ExtendedPhenotype` and `Turner2000ExtendedOrganism` support a biological analogy:

- organisms can project function into durable environmental structures
- built structures can become part of a larger maintenance or physiological regime

`TheraulazBonabeau1999Stigmergy` remains the closest ALIFE-facing bridge:

- agents leave traces in a shared environment
- those traces later constrain or enable behavior

`ClarkChalmers1998ExtendedMind` and `Hutchins1995CognitionInTheWild` support a second bridge:

- cognition can include durable external structures and symbol systems
- external scaffolds can become part of the operative cognitive loop

What this means for Quine:

- `environmental organ` is a strong paper-facing term, but it is **introduced terminology**, not established ALIFE jargon
- the term is safest when artifacts are not merely written, but later queried, reused, extended, or functionally relied upon
- `extended phenotype` and `extended organism` are useful analogies, especially for `3.1`, but should not dominate the front half

Implication for the paper:

- use `environmental organ` as a deliberate interpretive term anchored to extended phenotype, stigmergy, and scaffolded cognition
- do not write as if ALIFE already has a settled category with that exact name
- do not equate every persistent artifact with an environmental organ

## 3. The process view is useful, but not settled

Two papers are especially useful for keeping the rhetoric honest:

- `Austin2020OrganismsActivityBeing`
- `Morgan2022OrganismsSubstancesProcesses`

Their shared message is that the organism-as-process thesis is **contestable**, not settled biological doctrine.

Why this is good for Quine:

- it discourages overclaiming that process ontology is scientifically mandatory
- it encourages a narrower and stronger line: Quine's process-native ontology is a **productive explanatory stance** for this substrate

Recommended use:

- present process-native individuality as Quine's ontological commitment
- present its value in terms of what experiments it makes possible
- do not ask the ALIFE paper to win a general metaphysical dispute about whether organisms are really processes

## 3.5 Viability pressure versus selection pressure

The new terminology research also sharpens which pressure words the manuscript should use.

`DiPaolo2005AutopoiesisAdaptivityTeleologyAgency`, `BarandiaranDiPaoloRohde2009DefiningAgency`, and Beer's viability-oriented ALIFE work all support a real distinction:

- **selection pressure** is the familiar term when differential reproduction in a population is the center
- **viability constraint / viability pressure** is the cleaner term when the question is whether an individual or lineage can remain within life/death bounds

This matters because Quine's current conference paper is not yet a population-evolution paper.

The strongest paper-facing read is therefore:

- mortality, persistence, and maintenance are **viability pressures**
- those pressures can still be said to `select` or `recruit` different organizations in a looser ALIFE sense
- but the manuscript should avoid sounding as if it has already demonstrated Darwinian population selection

Implication for the paper:

- prefer `viability pressure` or `viability-relevant pressure` in the main framing
- reserve `selection pressure` for moments where the prose is explicitly broader or comparative
- if both appear, define the local sense clearly so the paper does not sound terminologically confused

## 4. Multi-agent systems and runtime architecture: where Quine actually differs

### Application-layer exemplars

`WuEtAl2023AutoGen` is a clean example of the dominant multi-agent paradigm:

- multiple agents converse
- behavior is programmed through conversation patterns and interaction behaviors
- the runtime abstraction remains framework-level

Official `LangGraph` documentation is useful as a non-paper comparator:

- nodes communicate by reading and writing shared state
- the public object model is graph nodes plus shared state, not native OS individuals

These are valuable systems, but they inhabit a different ontological layer from Quine.

### Closest systems comparator

`MeiEtAl2024AIOS` is the closest architectural near-neighbor.

Its contribution is to isolate scheduling, context management, memory management, storage management, and access control into an `AIOS kernel`, with an SDK for agent applications.

Why it matters:

- it means Quine cannot claim novelty for merely talking about operating-system-like agent support
- it gives a strong comparator for the `OS` language itself

Why Quine still differs:

- AIOS is an architecture for serving agent applications
- Quine's current ALIFE lane studies **native process lineages under real death and persistence conditions**
- Quine's paper-facing payoff is not agent serving efficiency, but process-native viability physics

## 5. What this implies for Quine's main claim

The safest three-layer stack is now:

1. **Ontological premise**
   Quine treats the individual as a native process rather than a framework-managed agent object.
2. **Methodological payoff**
   Because of that, mortality, persistence, and maintenance become substrate-level variables that can be perturbed separately enough for ALIFE experiments.
3. **Empirical demonstration**
   Those perturbations recruit bounded forms of continuity, ecological inheritance, and detached maintenance.

This produces a stronger and cleaner `so what` than either of the two weaker alternatives:

- weaker alternative A: `Quine is a novel ALIFE substrate`
- weaker alternative B: `Quine shows three cool life-like behaviors`

The stronger claim is:

- `Quine turns a process-native ontology into a controllable ALIFE method.`

After the new process-ontology and extended-phenotype research, that claim can be tightened further:

- Quine is not best framed as the first process-native ALIFE system in absolute terms
- it is better framed as a **rare process-native soft-ALIFE substrate** that makes process ontology experimentally consequential
- its strongest conceptual novelty is not ontology alone, but ontology translated into separable viability pressures and environmental organs

## 6. Novelty pockets worth pushing

Ordered by current defensibility:

1. **Process-native individuality**
   The individual is a process, not a framework object.
2. **Process-level death boundary**
   Death is process exit / termination, not only simulated episode bookkeeping.
3. **Filesystem as ecological medium**
   Persistence is not private memory but durable environmental state.
4. **Environmental organogenesis**
   Durable habitat can become a cognitive or maintenance organ.
5. **Detached maintenance**
   Upkeep can be externalized into post-exit structures under pressure.

Downstream but not yet conference-headline safe:

6. **Projected local world / subjective reality**
7. **Self-production, autopoiesis, or stronger autonomy**

## 7. Manuscript Consequences

### Safe use in the ALIFE paper

- one compact sentence in the introduction can state the process-native premise
- immediately cash it out into substrate facts: real death, volatile RAM, durable filesystem state, detached execution
- use `Pradeu2018GenidentityBiologicalProcesses` if the prose needs support for a methodological rather than grand-ontological process stance
- keep `Nicholson2018ReconceptualizingOrganism` and `Meincke2019AutopoiesisProcessView` as optional support for discussion or boundary-setting
- if `environmental organ` appears in the manuscript, anchor it implicitly or explicitly to `Dawkins1982ExtendedPhenotype`, `Turner2000ExtendedOrganism`, and `TheraulazBonabeau1999Stigmergy`
- if the prose needs a cognition-facing bridge, `ClarkChalmers1998ExtendedMind` and `Hutchins1995CognitionInTheWild` are better support than generic "external memory" language
- if pressure terminology needs discipline, use `DiPaolo2005AutopoiesisAdaptivityTeleologyAgency` and `BarandiaranDiPaoloRohde2009DefiningAgency` to justify `viability pressure` rather than relying only on evolutionary language

### Unsafe use in the ALIFE paper

- turning the introduction into a polemic against frameworks
- implying that process ontology is now a solved biological question
- using `autopoiesis` as a reward word for detached maintenance
- using `extended phenotype` or `environmental organ` as if they were already standard ALIFE categories for digital artifacts
- using `selection pressure` everywhere when the paper is really about organism- or lineage-level viability

## Red Lines

- do not let process ontology become the paper's headline instead of its engine
- do not argue that ordinary multi-agent systems are invalid because they are object-based
- do not treat autonomy / closure literature as if Quine had already met its bar
- do not claim novelty for `OS-like agent runtime` in the abstract; AIOS already occupies that lane
