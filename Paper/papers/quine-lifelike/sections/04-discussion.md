# Discussion

The central payoff of this work is methodological. Quine makes mortality, persistence, and maintenance experimentally separable in one process-native medium. The point of difference from adjacent ALIFE lines is not separability per se---classic digital ecologies, soft-ALIFE systems, and agent frameworks all offer tunable pressure parameters \citep{Ray1992SynthesisOfLife,OfriaWilke2004Avida,MordvintsevEtAl2020GrowingNCA,Chan2020LeniaExpandedUniverse,McMullin2004ThirtyYearsComputationalAutopoiesis}---but the level at which those pressures are enforced. In virtual-machine ecologies, pressures live inside the simulation's physics; in agent frameworks, lifecycle semantics are orchestrator policy. In Quine, the enforcement boundary is the operating-system kernel: death is process termination that destroys volatile state at the OS level, isolation is address-space separation that one generation cannot penetrate, persistence is real filesystem state that survives without framework mediation, and detached execution is a genuine background process rather than a registered callback. Because the substrate itself closes the door, the experimental contrast between pressure-on and pressure-off cannot be undermined by an implementation loophole \citep{Beer1995DynamicalSystems,Ke2026Quine}. The contribution is therefore not adjustability but substrate-enforced separability, which makes the causal attribution from manipulated pressure to recruited organization more secure than in nearby lines of work.

The three results matter not only for what they show individually, but for what they show together: the same medium can make mortality, persistence, and maintenance into independently assignable causes of organizational difference. Read as one answer to ALIFE's ``life as it could be'' question, the results support a bounded claim: a mortal computational lineage can realize experimentally tractable forms of life-like organization in a non-biological medium.

The continuity result matters first at the level of individuality. In Quine, the relevant individual is not a materially continuous process but a lineage that persists across real death boundaries through organized carryover. That matters because it relocates individuality from uninterrupted runtime to successful reconstruction after mortality. Relative to systems that identify the individual with a continuously running agent or with scheduler-level identity, Quine makes individuality something that has to be re-achieved generation by generation.

The environmental-cognition result matters at the level of inheritance. It shows that cognition and carryover can be mediated through durable habitat rather than genotype-like transmission \citep{OdlingSmeeEtAl2003NicheConstruction,TheraulazBonabeau1999Stigmergy}. What matters is not merely that artifacts survive, but that successor behavior changes when predecessor-modified structure persists and can be reused. In the clearest ablation, later generations inherit a usable carryover surface under persistence, lose that reuse when the surface is wiped, and fail to externalize it when the habitat is read-only. In that sense, the habitat is doing more than storing residue: it becomes part of the lineage's operative cognitive organization, and inheritance takes the form of usable world structure rather than of an internal memory handoff.

The maintenance result matters at the level of upkeep. It shows that maintenance can be offloaded into detached structures that continue restoring viability after the originating process exits \citep{ClarkChalmers1998ExtendedMind}. That is interesting because the substrate lets upkeep move across layers under experimental control: from no maintenance, to live maintenance, to post-exit maintenance. The point is not that Quine has reached maximal autonomy, but that it makes those maintenance-layer shifts directly testable.

Once the contribution is stated positively, the boundaries can be framed as questions of placement rather than retreat.

Relative to strong extended-organism or autopoiesis claims, the environmental-organ reading here remains narrower \citep{MaturanaVarela1980Autopoiesis,MossioMoreno2010OrganisationalClosure}. The cognition controls show why: writable persistence alone is not enough, birth is easier than explicit inherited uptake, and richer retrieval-organ ecologies remain more variable than the core carryover ablation. The maintenance result is likewise narrower than a strong autonomy claim, but stronger than a prompt-wording effect: under one prompt, longer realized disturbance horizons recruit more detached post-exit repair layers. More generally, all results are still short-horizon and single-lineage, so the paper stops before open-ended evolution or population-level selection. Appendix~A summarizes supporting replications and invalidated runs without burdening the main argument.

That scale boundary also clarifies the next horizon. The present paper establishes separable viability pressures at the level of a single mortal lineage: mortality, persistence, and maintenance become assignable causes of organizational difference within one lineage history.

The substrate vocabulary described in Section~2 points directly to the next scale. The lifecycle operators \texttt{fork}, \texttt{exec}, and \texttt{exit} are not metaphors for reproduction, metamorphosis, and death---they are the kernel-level operations through which computational individuals split, transform, and terminate. Exploratory continuation runs already suggest that these operators can be treated as experimental variables for reproductive continuation, branch competition, and lineage selection. Because the operating system already schedules concurrent processes and mediates shared filesystem access, multiple lineages can share a durable habitat without requiring a separate population manager. The same method that separates viability pressures for a single lineage can therefore, in principle, separate population-level pressures---resource competition, commons management, and lineage selection---within the same substrate.
