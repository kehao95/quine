# Conclusion

This paper asked whether a mortal computational lineage in a process-native medium can support bounded life-like organization under real viability pressure, and whether those pressures can be separated experimentally. The results support a clear yes to both questions.

The contribution to ALIFE is methodological. Quine provides a process-native habitat in which viability pressures are not merely adjustable but substrate-enforced: the operating-system kernel closes the enforcement boundary, so the causal attribution from manipulated pressure to recruited organization does not depend on orchestrator discipline.

The three contrasts reported here validate that claim. Real death recruits lineage persistence, persistent habitat recruits environmental cognitive organs, and longer maintenance horizons recruit more decoupled maintenance layers, each collapsing or weakening when its targeted pressure is ablated.

Together, those contrasts establish separable viability pressures at the level of a single mortal lineage. Because the same substrate supplies kernel-level lifecycle operators---\texttt{fork}, \texttt{exec}, \texttt{exit}---and native process scheduling, the next scale of questions on reproductive continuation, branch competition, and population-level viability dynamics is reachable without leaving the medium.
