# Dossier: Life-Like Organization

**Status: Current canonical framing** (promoted 2026-04-02)

This dossier captures the now-canonical paper center.

## Core Question

Can a mortal computational lineage realize bounded life-like organization—continuity, ecological inheritance, and self-maintenance—under real viability pressure? And under which habitat conditions does that organization appear or collapse?

## Core Claim

Quine matters not primarily because it inhabits a novel substrate, but because it is a concrete case where bounded life-like organizations become experimentally visible. Under real viability pressure in a UNIX/POSIX habitat, a mortal LLM-driven process lineage can realize continuity across death boundaries, ecological inheritance through durable traces, and detached post-exit maintenance.

## What It Foregrounds

- ALIFE's `life as it could be` question as the paper's entry point
- continuity, inheritance, and self-maintenance as the real headline
- Quine as one bounded answer rather than one more architecture
- the operating-system habitat as what makes those claims experimentally legible

## What It Plays Down

- substrate novelty as title-level center
- long defense of why UNIX/POSIX is interesting in itself
- engineering-platform rhetoric
- broad ontology claims such as `Quine is alive`

## Language Rule

- broad gloss: `Quine can behave in life-like ways`
- safer manuscript formulation: `Quine realizes bounded life-like organization`
- avoid making `behaves like life` the sole technical sentence; it needs the
  bounded organizational follow-through

## Main Risks

- can sound overclaimed if `life-like` outruns the actual evidence ladder
- can slide into rhetorical warmth without enough mechanistic specificity
- can understate the methodological importance of the habitat if substrate is
  played down too aggressively
