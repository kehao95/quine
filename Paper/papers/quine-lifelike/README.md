# Separable Viability Pressures in a Process-Native ALIFE Habitat

## Paper Identity

**Framing: Life-Like Organization** (promoted 2026-04-02; see [`dossiers/README.md`](dossiers/README.md))

ALIFE asks what life could be in other media. This paper answers that question with a concrete case: Quine, a mortal LLM-driven process lineage, can realize bounded forms of continuity, ecological inheritance, and self-maintenance under real viability pressure. The paper matters not because the substrate is novel, but because these life-like organizations become experimentally visible there.
Current best posture: **hybrid empirical-paper + experimental-substrate framing**. The paper remains a main-conference empirical ALIFE submission, but Quine should now be presented as the controllable substrate that makes the paper's three pressure-sensitive results scientifically useful.

## Current Question

Which lineage-level organizations can be selected by independently manipulated mortality, persistence, and maintenance pressures in Quine as a controllable process-native ALIFE habitat? And under which habitat contrasts do those organizations appear, strengthen, or collapse?

## Thesis

ALIFE's central question is not only how life works as we know it, but what life-like organization could be in other media. This paper investigates that question in Quine, treated not only as a concrete case but as a controllable process-native ALIFE habitat in which mortality, persistence, and maintenance can be manipulated independently. In this UNIX habitat, death is host-enforced, volatile state is lost across generations, and durable traces may or may not persist. Those conditions make viability-relevant pressures experimentally tractable rather than metaphorical.

The upstream philosophical distinction matters here as an enabling premise rather than as the paper's headline. Quine treats the individual as a native process rather than a framework-managed agent object. That process-native ontology is what makes death, reset, persistence, and detached maintenance substrate facts instead of orchestration conventions.

The paper's empirical contribution is that three distinct bounded organizations are recruited under those pressures: continuity across death boundaries, ecological inheritance through predecessor-modified traces, and detached post-exit maintenance under stronger maintenance pressure. The `so what` is therefore methodological as well as empirical: Quine gives ALIFE a synthetic habitat where lineage-level organizations can be separated, manipulated, and compared through controlled contrasts, not only described after the fact.

These three readouts are the current paper's bounded center, not Quine's full ALIFE ceiling. Source-level review now fixes a broader reusable conclusion in the control plane: once individuality is process-native, the same substrate also exposes computational life cycles, branching local worlds, signaling ecology, budgeted commons, and cognitive metabolism as downstream ALIFE directions. Those directions belong in discussion/outlook or later papers unless they become direct evidence in the conference ladder.
The paper-family decision is now fixed: this dossier remains the conference submission lane, while broader habitat/systematization work is deferred to the separate `quine-process-habitat` reservoir for later journal-scale development.

## Prose Style: Public Paper vs Personal Notes

**Problem**: First drafts often read like personal notes or rebuttal letters rather than confident academic prose. Symptoms include:

- Defensive section titles like "Why X Belongs in Y" (breaks fourth wall)
- Explicit justification sentences like "This paper takes that stance because..." (meta-commentary)
- Self-referential claims like "The claim in this subsection is..." (outline scaffolding leaked into prose)
- Excessive disclaimers like "We are not claiming X, nor Y, nor Z" (arguing with imaginary critics)

**Fix**: Write as if the work's place in the field is already settled:

| Anti-pattern | Repair |
|--------------|--------|
| `Quine belongs in ALIFE because...` | `Aligning with this tradition, Quine serves as...` |
| `This paper takes that methodological stance directly` | `Building on this foundation, the present work isolates...` |
| `The claim in this subsection is...` | `In summary:` (single sentence, end of subsection) |
| `Why This Belongs in ALIFE` (title) | `Quine as Synthetic Experimental Substrate` |

**Rule**: Use field concepts and vocabulary to define the work; let readers infer disciplinary fit rather than being told.

## Terminology: Precision and Audience Calibration

**Problem**: Cross-disciplinary writing often accumulates imprecise or audience-mismatched terminology:

- Redundant combinations (e.g., "UNIX/POSIX") signal uncertainty about which term is correct
- Over-explaining concepts the target audience already knows (e.g., "non-biological" for ALIFE readers)
- Under-explaining concepts the target audience doesn't know (e.g., "process" properties for non-CS readers)
- Undefined jargon that assumes shared context (e.g., "host" without clarifying it means operating system)
- Unnecessarily technical vocabulary that obscures rather than clarifies (e.g., "written" vs "modified")

**Principles**:

| Principle | Explanation | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Precision over hedging** | Choose the most accurate single term; don't combine alternatives | `UNIX/POSIX` → `UNIX` |
| **Concrete over abstract** | Specificity beats generality for the target venue | `non-biological substrate` → `UNIX process lineage` |
| **Define on first use** | Clarify terms that cross domain boundaries | `host-enforced` → `OS-enforced` (with "host operating system" on first occurrence) |
| **Explain relevant properties only** | For cross-disciplinary concepts, explain only the attributes that matter to the argument | For processes: "isolated, finite-lived, terminable" — not memory management or scheduling |
| **Minimize CS jargon** | Prefer words that carry meaning in both domains | `written` → `modified` |

**Test**: For each technical term, ask: (1) Does the target audience need this explained? (2) Does this audience already know this and find explanation patronizing? (3) Is there a more domain-neutral equivalent?
