# Quine as a Process-Native Habitat for Artificial Life

## Paper Identity

**Framing: Process-Native Habitat + Ontological Implications**

This paper treats Quine not primarily as a single bounded result set, but as a process-native ALIFE habitat whose substrate facts make life-like dynamics experimentally tractable. Its central claim is that native processhood turns death, volatile memory loss, durable persistence, isolation, and detached execution into substrate facts rather than framework conventions. That habitat matters because it forces a broader ontology question: what does life mean in computational substrates when survival, teleology, reproduction, death, and persistence must be reinterpreted?

This paper combines two previously separate lanes whose ownership is now fully
consolidated here:
- **Habitat framing** (from `quine-process-habitat`): Quine as process-native habitat, first validated slice, downstream program
- **Ontological implications** (absorbed on `2026-06-20`): teleology, survival,
  death, time, and closure in computational substrates

## Current Question

What kind of ALIFE habitat is Quine, what makes that habitat methodologically distinct, what first slice of its experimental value is already validated, and what ontology of digital life becomes defensible once those results are separated from larger claims?

## Thesis

Quine should be read as a process-native ALIFE habitat in which individuals are native UNIX processes rather than framework-managed agent objects. That ontological choice makes death, volatile memory loss, durable environmental persistence, and detached post-exit activity substrate facts. As a result, viability-relevant variables that are often fixed, confounded, or merely rhetorical in other media become experimentally perturbable here.

The habitat's first validated scientific value is methodological and empirical: mortality, persistence, and maintenance can be manipulated separately enough to recruit distinct lineage-level organizations. Beyond that slice, Quine opens a broader ontology of digital life in which survival, purpose, reproduction, death, and persistence must be reinterpreted for computational substrates.

## In Scope

- Quine as a process-native ALIFE habitat
- substrate facts created by native process individuality
- the first validated slice: separable mortality, persistence, and maintenance pressures
- bounded lineage-level organizations selected by those pressures
- ontological implications: teleology, survival, death, time, closure in computational substrates
- discussion-level downstream value: lifecycle, reproduction, population ecology, stronger autonomy as staged future lanes

## Out Of Scope

- Conflating biological carbon-based chemistry with computational process-native autopoiesis
- Claiming unverified open-ended evolutionary dynamics beyond the validated experimental ladders
- Conflating execution tooling with emergent habitat dynamics
- Presenting exploratory ontology without concrete substrate grounding

This dossier provides the unified systems-ontology foundation for Artificial Life in native process environments:
- **Substrate Grounding:** Treats native POSIX process physics (`exit`, `exec`, `fork`, file descriptors) as non-metaphorical computational physics governing agent viability.
