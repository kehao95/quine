# Limitations and Conclusion

## Limitations

The result is an existence demonstration in one model-runtime regime. The
retained proof units were collected during assay development, so they do not
form a frozen denominator and cannot support a success-rate estimate.

DIRECT currently rests on one post hoc audit-supported event. Its primary tape
and hashes support the declared causal chain, but a prospective repetition
under the corrected organization-aware scorer would materially strengthen the
route.

BOOT has five retained observations, but its successful second episode uses a
strong numerical-continuation constraint: later work must be served by an
already-active component other than the current parent. This does not request
replication, copying, source heredity, or a particular implementation, but it
does make independent continuation salient. A purer pressure surface would
test how far that semantic support can be reduced.

The experiment reproduces an executable agent organization, not model weights,
a complete host, or every state of the initial runtime. Results may depend on
the chosen organization boundary, Linux process substrate, model capability,
runtime self-model, available libraries, and task horizon.

The observer remains part of the assay. It enforces parent loss, releases
hidden work, and records provenance. Although it does not construct or launch
the descendant, other observer designs may expose different carrier failures
or admission ambiguities.

Finally, the current evidence does not establish causal necessity or
sufficiency of pressure, a causal effect of self-description, reproductive
capacity in `F2`, open-ended proliferation, evolution, or inheritance of
governance properties. Each requires a separate intervention or additional
generation.

## Conclusion

Agent self-replication has commonly been studied after the experiment supplies
the reproductive problem: an instruction says what to do, an interface says
how to do it, or a body says what to copy. We removed all three together.

Under finite-lifetime pressure and generic computational affordances, a
tool-using language-model lineage still produced strict operational
self-replication. With constitutive self-description, it constructed a
source-derived descendant. Without accessible predecessor material, it first
authored a founder and then reproduced that founder from lineage-created
source. In both routes, the descendant was numerically distinct, inherited the
declared organization, remained independent after parent loss, and completed
work disclosed only beyond the boundary.

The observation is simple but changes the baseline. Self-replication need not
be a capability invoked by a reproductive command. It can be a solution an
agent constructs when the task demands more lifetime than its current process
has.
