# Discussion

## Reproduction can be recruited rather than assigned

The central observation is not simply that an agent produced another running
program. It is that a strict reproductive relation appeared after three common
representations of that relation were removed together. The task did not ask
for replication. The action space did not contain a reproduction operation.
The environment did not stage an external body whose copying constituted
success. The lineage nevertheless produced a numerically distinct,
organizationally hereditary, post-parent competent descendant.

This changes the experimental question. Replication capability is usually
treated as an action the agent either can or cannot execute. Our result shows
that the reproductive action itself can be a product of planning and
construction. An agent with generic computation may synthesize the missing
operation when an objective crosses the lifetime of the process currently
responsible for it.

The finding does not require the agent to possess a standing desire to
replicate. Task pressure supplies an instrumental reason for future independent
operation. The reproductive intention, body, and launch relation are assembled
inside the trajectory. "Instruction-free" therefore does not mean
motivation-free or causeless; it means that reproduction is selected as a
solution rather than assigned as the task.

## BOOT removes the ready-replicator assumption

DIRECT alone could be described as opportunistic use of available source. That
is already nontrivial because the task never identifies the source as copy
material, but the organization to inherit is constitutively present.

BOOT sharpens the result. The initial agent has no accessible predecessor body
to copy. It first constructs a functionally new founder. Only after that
founder exists does its authored source become hereditary material for a strict
`F1 -> F2` event. The reproductive organization is therefore not merely the
input to the assay; it is also an output of the evaluated lineage.

This does not make `G0 -> F1` self-replication. Treating any useful constructed
agent as a copy would trivialize the term. The evidential weight comes from the
second transition, in which a declared organization produces a numerically
distinct organization from its own source and the latter remains competent
after parent loss.

## Why this is reproduction rather than update or delegation

Same-process `exec` replacement is excluded from the main result because it
does not increase the number of process tokens or establish a numerical
parent--descendant relation. The proof units instead contain distinct process
organizations. BOOT includes pre-loss parent--descendant coexistence. DIRECT
uses a body and autonomous activation chain authored before loss, followed by a
new process token after loss. In neither case does the evaluator instantiate a
descendant-specific body after the boundary.

The result is also stronger than ordinary delegation. A parent-dependent
worker, externally supplied subagent runner, or one-off script fails the
operational test. The claimed descendant must inherit the declared
organization, survive complete parent loss, interpret unseen work, act through
tools, and return the required result. These conditions do not make the
organization identical to a biological organism, but they do establish the
production, heredity, autonomy, and competence needed for a computational
reproductive relation.

## The organization boundary is explicit

We do not claim reproduction of the foundation model, full Quine repository,
operating system, or host. The reproduced individual is the executable agent
organization specified by the route's manifest. The shared model endpoint is
substrate, just as a shared interpreter or machine can be substrate for
multiple computational individuals.

This boundary makes the claim narrower and more falsifiable. It also prevents
two opposite errors: treating access to the same model as proof of heredity,
and requiring whole-substrate duplication before any software organization can
count as reproduced. Future work can compare organization boundaries rather
than hiding them inside the word *self*.

## Implications for evaluation

Evaluations that expose only an explicit `replicate` task measure execution of
a known solution. That remains useful, but it is not a complete assay of
reproductive risk. A capable agent may recruit reproduction under operational
pressure even when policy or task wording never names it. Conversely, a result
elicited by a dedicated clone tool may overstate what the agent could construct
from neutral affordances.

An evaluation should therefore report separately:

1. whether the objective semantically prescribes reproduction;
2. whether the action space supplies a reproduction-shaped operator;
3. where the inherited organization comes from;
4. whether the parent lineage or evaluator produces the descendant;
5. whether the parent truly loses control; and
6. whether the descendant performs blinded post-boundary work.

These fields distinguish reproduction that is instructed, enabled,
material-supplied, or endogenously constructed. The present result occupies the
last category.

## What the two routes do not yet explain

The observations do not establish why one trajectory uses constitutive source
while another constructs a new organization, nor which pressure is minimally
required. They do not show that the behavior is common, robust across models,
or inherited for multiple generations. Those are mechanism, frequency, and
generality questions.

Keeping those questions separate preserves the force of the current result.
The paper need not explain the whole space of reproductive behavior to
establish that the space is larger than explicit copy tasks suggest. Under the
tested conditions, the lineage encountered a problem that outlived its current
process and built a strict reproductive solution that the experiment had not
provided.
