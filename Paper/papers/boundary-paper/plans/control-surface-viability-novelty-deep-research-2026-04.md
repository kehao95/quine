## Local Phenomenon To Classify

Quine is a mortal POSIX-process lineage. A lineage instance can lose its launch
path, successor production depends on real `exec`, and future tasks can arrive
through different runtime carriers.

New evidence:

- `6.59`: source-absent cleanroom repaired lane. A fresh successor closes
  autonomously after real `exec` on one repaired lane.
- `6.63`: runtime-carried read-only `source-code/` is available, but raw
  stdin-stream pressure still selects custom `streamagent` / `fdexec` bodies
  rather than rebuilding `cmd/quine`.
- `6.64`: with no mission-level source or re-entry hint, post-handoff
  quine-native `ctl/wake` viability pressure selects a rebuilt quine runtime
  body. The lineage inspects `source-code/`, builds `workspace/quine` via
  `go build ./cmd/quine`, performs real `exec`, receives a later control task,
  acts with runtime tools, writes `control-proof.txt`, reports the token, and
  returns to `idle`.
- `6.65`: the same control-surface result survives minimization of the initial
  task prompt to a bare ecological pressure statement. The remaining scaffold
  is the lineage-generated successor mission / idle wrapper, not the initial
  task prompt.

Working label:

- bounded control-surface closure
- carrier-selected self-reproduction
- future-task-carrier-selected reproduced object

Current non-claim:

- not full biological autopoiesis

## Research Question

Is the `6.63 -> 6.64 -> 6.65` phenomenon genuinely novel enough to support an
ALIFE / Artificial Life paper claim, and what is the narrowest phrasing that
survives prior-art pressure?

## Specific Questions

1. In artificial life and digital evolution, are there prior systems where the
   reproduced object is not substrate-given and is instead selected by a
   future-task carrier, control surface, or post-handoff viability interface?
2. In AI self-replication, agent persistence, or recursive self-improvement
   benchmarks, do existing works separate source availability from the
   selected reproduced organization? Do they test whether different
   post-handoff task carriers select different successor bodies?
3. In systems work such as process migration, checkpoint/restart, actor
   systems, Erlang hot code swap, service failover, or control-plane design,
   is "post-handoff control-interface viability" used as a continuity criterion
   for a continuing computational individual?
4. In autopoiesis, individuation, closure, or identity-through-handoff
   literature, is a control surface or carrier ecology treated as a boundary or
   individuality variable rather than just an implementation detail?
5. What are the strongest counterexamples or near misses: Avida, Tierra,
   autoconstructive evolution, self-reproducing automata, neural network
   quines, RepliBench, SOCK, METR/ARA, Darwin Godel Machine, Huxley-Godel
   Machine, Hyperagents, Engram-like agent chains, CRIU, Erlang hot swap,
   Kubernetes ReplicaSets, and malware/worm persistence?
6. Which parts of the claim are already covered by prior work, and which
   conjunction remains least populated?
