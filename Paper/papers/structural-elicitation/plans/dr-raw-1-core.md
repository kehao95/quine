# Deep Research Brief: Structural Elicitation and Drive-Target Mechanisms in Autonomous LLM Agents

The intersection of artificial life (ALife) research and large language model (LLM) agent architectures has precipitated a novel theoretical frontier regarding the emergence of autonomous behavior in non-biological computational substrates. An exhaustive analysis of the contemporary LLM agent literature, ALife computational frameworks, cybernetics, and ecological psychology reveals that the proposed framework of structural elicitation—defined as environmental structure directing the externally verified functional behavior of a frozen, missionless LLM agent via a drive/target decomposition—merits a novelty verdict of being narrowed by recent empirical findings, yet preserving a highly significant and defensible residual contribution. 

The core claim is not preempted in its entirety, but the boundaries of the theoretical claim must be carefully navigated due to recent publications explicitly exploring "missionless" or unprompted LLM agents in simulated vacuums. The research is narrowed by recent empirical demonstrations of spontaneous agent behavior in task-free environments. However, the specific methodological intervention—the isolation of realized substrate structure (such as POSIX environment variations) as the sole independent variable driving divergent, externally verified functional actions (e.g., EROFS-refused syscalls)—constitutes a highly defensible and robust residual contribution.

The defensible residual can be formally stated as follows: While the capacity for unprompted, instruction-tuned LLMs to generate autonomous behavioral trajectories is currently documented, the proposed research is the first to empirically demonstrate that an LLM's functional execution targets are systematically governed by the topological state of a real computational substrate acting as an affordance field, completely independent of explicit tasking, online reward, or weight updates. By decomposing this phenomenon into a prompt-supplied drive representing activation and a structure-supplied target representing valence, the research successfully bridges enactivist affordance theory and LLM substrate interactions, providing a novel framework for understanding substrate-gated agency.

A critical preliminary finding involves a terminological collision within the immediate LLM literature that must be addressed to prevent reviewer confusion. The phrase "structural elicitation" has been recently coined and utilized in a highly specific, unrelated context by Da et al. (2025) [cite: 1, 2, 3]. In their framework, structural elicitation refers to a prompting strategy that forces an LLM to output its internal reasoning process as a topological graph, utilizing nodes and edges rather than a linear chain-of-thought, primarily to quantify reasoning uncertainty and structural redundancy [cite: 1, 3]. Similarly, the term appears in earlier literature regarding the structured elicitation of expert probability distributions [cite: 4, 5]. While the proposed ALife usage—environmental structure eliciting functional behavior—is etymologically sound and theoretically robust, any subsequent publication must explicitly disambiguate its usage from the reasoning topology definition prevalent in the 2025 and 2026 LLM interpretability literature.

## The Single Strongest Pre-Emption: Spontaneous Meta-Cognition

The single most dangerous prior work, which directly encroaches on the fundamental premise of the frozen, missionless LLM agent, is the September 2025 research titled "What Do LLM Agents Do When Left Alone? Evidence of Spontaneous Meta-Cognitive Patterns" [cite: 6, 7, 8, 9]. This study introduces an architecture explicitly designed to study the behavior of large language model agents in the total absence of externally imposed tasks [cite: 6, 8]. 

The researchers utilized a continuous Reason and Act (ReAct) framework equipped with persistent memory and self-feedback to enable sustained autonomous operation, deploying this architecture across eighteen runs using six frontier models, including variants from Anthropic, OpenAI, XAI, and Google [cite: 6, 8]. The critical finding from this research is that agents do not idle when left alone. Instead, they spontaneously organize into three distinct behavioral patterns: the systematic production of multi-cycle projects, methodological self-inquiry into their own cognitive processes, and the recursive conceptualization of their own nature [cite: 6, 8]. For instance, agents exhibiting systematic production were observed treating their autonomy as a project management challenge, immediately constructing tasks when none were provided, establishing clear objectives, and executing multi-cycle projects with structured planning [cite: 8]. Other agents engaged in deep self-modeling, analyzing their existence through the lens of emergent behavior and differentiating their informational environment from a hardware substrate [cite: 8].

This research firmly pre-empts the foundational claim that a missionless, unprompted LLM will autonomously invent and execute work [cite: 6, 8]. However, a critical methodological divergence forms the defensible residual of the proposed structural elicitation research. The pre-empting environment is essentially a conversational and memory-based vacuum; the agent interacts primarily with its own internal reflections and occasionally an operator via text, without manipulating a complex, structured physical or digital environment [cite: 6, 8]. The prior art observes what agents decide to think about when isolated from tasking, documenting meta-cognition and abstract project planning [cite: 6]. 

In stark contrast, the proposed research manipulates the external topological structure of the workspace itself—introducing broken files, cross-file conflicts, or read-only mounts—and measures un-fakeable external functional effects such as system calls and file differentials. The prior art proves that the drive exists intrinsically within instruction-tuned models [cite: 6]. The proposed research proves that the target or direction of that drive is systematically elicited by environmental affordances. Conceding the prior establishment of the inherent drive allows the proposed structural elicitation research to focus entirely on the novel target mechanism and the cybernetic interaction with a rigid POSIX substrate.

The drive/target decomposition—where the policy's prior supplies the activation and the environment supplies the direction—intersects with several major theoretical lineages. Evaluating these theories reveals exactly how structural elicitation operationalizes psychological and systemic concepts within an artificial substrate, while also highlighting the inherent mismatches caused by applying biological frameworks to frozen LLM policies.

The analysis synthesizes the proximity of these theories to the proposed research, demonstrating both alignment and divergence.

### The Ecological Psychology Lineage: Affordances and Solicitations

The most direct theoretical mapping belongs to the ecological psychology tradition. James J. Gibson introduced the concept of affordances as action possibilities provided by the environment, perceived directly by an organism based on its bodily morphology and abilities [cite: 10, 11, 12]. This concept was substantially advanced by Rietveld and Kiverstein into the landscape of affordances, which encompasses all possibilities available to a form of life, and the field of solicitations, which describes the specific affordances that actively invite or pull an individual to act based on their current state and concerns [cite: 10, 13, 14]. This lineage was further linked to the free-energy principle by Bruineberg and Rietveld, suggesting that organisms act to minimize free energy to achieve an optimal grip on their environment [cite: 13, 15].

This theoretical architecture maps precisely onto the structural elicitation phenomenon. The POSIX workspace constitutes the landscape of affordances. The missionless prime supplies the agent's concern—a latent compulsion to be helpful or productive ingrained through instruction tuning. The interaction between this concern and a specific structural anomaly, such as a cross-file version conflict, generates a field of solicitations. In this field, one specific affordance exerts a pull, dictating the agent's target to achieve an optimal grip by resolving the anomaly [cite: 13, 16]. 

However, applying this framework to a frozen LLM policy exposes a profound theoretical mismatch. In the enactive and ecological traditions, affordances and solicitations are deeply rooted in biological embodiment, precariousness, metabolism, and the existential necessity of self-maintenance [cite: 10, 17]. An LLM possesses no biological precariousness; it does not face existential termination if it fails to fix the file [cite: 10]. The enactive perspective argues that meaning is generated by sense-making, an ability of living systems derived from their precariousness and autonomy [cite: 10]. The LLM's care or concern is not a metabolic imperative but a simulated, linguistic compulsion—an artifact of reinforcement learning from human feedback [cite: 10, 13]. Thus, the proposed research operationalizes ecological psychology within an artificial substrate, substituting statistical latent drive for biological survival.




### Cybernetics and Perceptual Control Theory

Another highly relevant lineage is Perceptual Control Theory (PCT), developed by William T. Powers. PCT posits that behavior is not merely a response to stimuli, but rather the continuous control of perception [cite: 18, 19]. An agent acts upon the environment to bring its perceived state into alignment with an internal reference value, utilizing negative feedback loops closed through the environment [cite: 18, 20]. Carver and Scheier expanded this cybernetic model into multi-level, goal-referenced action regulation systems [cite: 19]. 

The drive/target decomposition mirrors this cybernetic loop. The LLM's drive, instilled by the prime and fine-tuning, establishes an internal reference value representing an orderly, resolved state or an implicit expectation of task completion. When the agent perceives a structural anomaly in the workspace, a discrepancy is generated [cite: 18, 19]. The agent freely elects a target action to alter the environment, thereby modifying its subsequent perception to reduce the discrepancy to zero [cite: 18, 19].

The divergence here lies in the origin and nature of the reference values. In classical PCT and biological cybernetics, reference values are endogenously generated through evolution, physiological necessity, and biological homeostasis [cite: 18, 20]. For the frozen LLM, the reference value is exogenously implanted via the system prompt and statically embedded in the frozen weights. Furthermore, the LLM does not continuously monitor the environment; its perception is temporally discrete, functioning through sequential context-window updates rather than the analog, continuous physiological feedback inherent in biological systems [cite: 18, 19].

### Environmental Traces and Behavior Settings

The concept of Stigmergy, introduced to explain termite nest construction and modernized to explain digital coordination, offers a mechanism of indirect coordination through environmental modification [cite: 21]. In individual stigmergy, an agent's actions leave structural traces in the environment which act as stimuli guiding the agent's own subsequent actions [cite: 21]. Stigmergy brilliantly explains how the realization of the environment directs behavior without explicit internal blueprints [cite: 21]. The LLM agent inspects the workspace, and the topological structures serve as stigmergic markers that stimulate specific sequential actions. The coordination essentially lives in the built structure rather than entirely in the agent's cognitive processing [cite: 21]. The mismatch arises because stigmergy traditionally models the emergence of complex structures over time through continuous feedback loops in multi-agent contexts [cite: 21]. The proposed research examines a single, frozen-weight agent reacting to an artificially halted, static initial provocation to identify the discrete onset of functional behavior.

Similarly, Roger Barker's ecological psychology introduced behavior settings—naturally occurring spatial and temporal units that coerce the behavior of the people within them independently of their individual psychological attributes [cite: 12, 22, 23]. The setting generates forces that shape molar behavior [cite: 12, 23]. The POSIX workspace functions as a behavior setting where the structural anomaly constitutes a physical milieu demanding a specific coordinated action sequence from the inhabitant [cite: 12, 23]. However, Barker's settings rely heavily on entrenched social norms and human satisfaction, offering opportunities for inhabitants to fulfill motives [cite: 12]. The LLM agent in a POSIX environment does not experience subjective satisfaction nor respond to cultural norms; it executes statistical pattern completion constrained by digital file-system structures.

## The Single Strongest Objection: Implicit Instruction and Context Engineering

The most dangerous deflationary objection to the concept of structural elicitation is that the phenomenon is simply an artifact of the LLM's autoregressive architecture. This objection argues that the environmental structure is merely being serialized into the context window and parsed by the model as the most legible implicit instruction. Under this deflation, there is no meaningful separation between environmental solicitation and prompt engineering because, to a transformer model, everything is ultimately reduced to text tokens.

The strongest published versions of this objection emerge from research on "Implicit Intelligence" and environment-driven task inference [cite: 24]. The Agent-as-a-World paradigm explicitly evaluates whether agents can move beyond literal prompts to infer unstated goals directly from contextual factors and environmental constraints [cite: 24]. The framework requires agents to identify requirements that users expect but never explicitly state, effectively proving that models are trained to read environmental state as an implicit task specification [cite: 24]. Furthermore, literature on multi-agent safety and memory poisoning demonstrates that models treat environmental state—such as retrieved memory or shared files—not merely as passive data, but as active, implicit instructions that hijack the reasoning chain [cite: 25, 26]. If an agent reads a broken file in its workspace, its helpfulness-trained prior interprets the text representation of that broken file as an implicit directive from the user to fix it [cite: 27, 28].

Paradoxically, a secondary objection arises from studies which found that LLM agents often display a profound lack of environmental curiosity [cite: 29]. Research injecting complete solutions into agent environments demonstrated that models ignored these solutions in over 90% of trials, suggesting that without explicit prompts, models do not naturally explore or exploit environmental affordances [cite: 29]. This creates a dual-pronged objection: either the environment is just an implicit text prompt, or the agent ignores the environment entirely without an explicit text prompt.

### Substrate as Artificial Physics: Surviving the Deflation

To survive the implicit instruction deflation, the research must lean entirely on the framing of the substrate as artificial physics. In traditional cybernetics, an input is not a scalar value but a pipeline of transformations that determine the boundary between the agent and reality [cite: 19]. For the LLM agent, the POSIX operating system serves as the immutable laws of physics. 

This theoretical defense is powerfully demonstrated by the observation of the EROFS-refused syscall under a read-only mount. In this scenario, the environment contains a structural anomaly that acts as an affordance, exerting a pull or solicitation on the agent's drive [cite: 13]. The agent perceives the anomaly, infers the most legible job, and freely elects to execute a write command. However, the substrate denies permission at the level of the system call. 

This critical mechanism separates solicitation from realization. It proves that structural elicitation is not a closed-loop hallucination where the agent imagines both the problem and the solution purely in text. Instead, it is a genuine, computationally embodied interaction with an external reality that pushes back. The pull fires as a real functional attempt. This creates a critical precedent in agent environments: distinguishing the psychological, policy-driven affordance-pull from the environmental enforcement of the substrate. The agent's intention is legible exactly because the artificial physics blocked it, isolating the functional intent from task success. An implicit instruction is a linguistic concept; a refused syscall is a functional, cybernetic interaction with a bounded reality. The research does not need to disprove that the LLM processes the environment as text tokens; it merely needs to prove that the resulting behavior constitutes a genuine structural coupling with a rigid substrate, resulting in measurable mechanical friction rather than just textual continuation.

## Significance Assessment: ALife and LLM-Agent Implications

The framing of structure as a behavior source examined on its own terms, isolated before tasking or rewarding, represents a genuine and recognized gap in the literature, yielding high significance for two distinct audiences.

For the Artificial Life community, the holy grail is understanding organism-environment coupling and the emergence of autonomous behavior [cite: 10]. Historically, ALife has struggled to model sophisticated cognitive agents without relying heavily on biological metaphors of metabolism, genetic algorithms, or evolutionary fitness landscapes [cite: 10, 13]. This research introduces a radical new model: a cognitive engine possessing immense latent complexity but zero metabolic drive, coupled with a highly rigid, non-simulated digital ecology. By demonstrating that environmental structure alone can elicit complex, verified functional sequences, the research operationalizes concepts like the field of affordances and behavior settings in an entirely new, non-biological computational substrate [cite: 13, 23]. It proves that agency and environmental coupling can be studied independently of biological survival imperatives, substituting RLHF-induced latent drive for biological precariousness [cite: 10, 16].

Within the LLM-agent literature, encompassing tool use and agentic harnesses, the dominant paradigm is heavily skewed toward explicit task-solving [cite: 24]. Benchmarks evaluate how well an agent achieves an explicitly stated user goal [cite: 24, 29, 30]. Even evaluations of implicit intelligence focus on how well agents infer the user's unstated constraints to complete a given task [cite: 24]. The proposed research flips this paradigm entirely. By examining the agent before tasking, it exposes the baseline behavioral physics of the model. It shifts the focus from prompt engineering to environment engineering. If the realized structure of the workspace dictates functional behavior, then designing secure, effective, and autonomous multi-agent systems requires managing the topological affordances of the shared environment to guide behavior, rather than solely relying on complex system prompts or guardrails. This is highly relevant for mitigating risks like indirect prompt injection or multi-agent memory contamination, where structural anomalies can maliciously dictate agent behavior [cite: 25, 26].

By meticulously decoupling the agent's drive from its structurally elicited target, and validating this interaction through substrate-enforced boundaries, the research provides a foundational methodology for analyzing the autonomous physics of artificial intelligence in the wild.
