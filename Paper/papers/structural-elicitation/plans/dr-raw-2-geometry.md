# The Beyond-LBA Theory: Structural-Valence Cartography and Environment-as-Geometry

The theoretical framework under evaluation represents a fundamental extension beyond established linear behavioral models, asserting a dual-layer organizational logic for autonomous, instruction-tuned agents operating within complex, unbounded environments such as Portable Operating System Interface (POSIX) workspaces. The context of this analysis is a frozen, missionless Large Language Model (LLM) agent interacting with a live operating system, where structural environments direct externally verified functional behaviors. The proposed cartography bifurcates into two interdependent models: Layer A, which establishes a structural-valence matrix of vector forces, and Layer B, which conceptualizes the environment as a multi-dimensional geometry and thermodynamic landscape.

The core research question necessitates a rigorous interrogation of the structural novelty of this framework. It asks whether these two organizing moves—the crossing of a two-grammar mechanism against an abstraction ladder utilizing reference-inversion, alongside an environment-as-geometry framework defined by five non-vectorial meta-dimensions—constitute a genuinely novel organizational instrument. Alternatively, the analysis must determine the extent to which existing theoretical frameworks already occupy this territory. Through an exhaustive review of adjacent literature spanning ecological psychology, cybernetics, systems engineering, and cognitive science, this report isolates the dominant pre-emptions and articulates the precise defensible residual of the proposed theory.

## Layer A: The Structural-Valence Matrix and Vector Forces

Layer A organizes the operational vectors of a frozen agent through a two-axis matrix. One axis consists of the mechanism grammar, which proposes two strictly independent and co-equal drivers of behavior: discrepancy resolution (the imperative to repair, realize, or normalize a broken or inconsistent state) and affordance solicitation (the uptake of an open, legible, and low-friction action surface). The other axis consists of a five-layer abstraction and coupling ladder ascending from form and syntax, through topology and semantics, to operational dynamics and the global field. Grounding this matrix is the concept of reference-inversion, where the comparator state against which a discrepancy is measured is inferred directly from the static structure via the policy's amortized prior, entirely devoid of an external reward or value signal.

### Crossing Mechanism Across an Abstraction Ladder

The proposition of an abstraction ladder—where the operative level of action is made visible to the agent by structural breakdown, and where attention-cost rises in tandem with the level of abstraction—has deep and well-theorized roots in cognitive and systems engineering. The nearest adjacent frameworks occupying this territory are Activity Theory and Ecological Interface Design.

Originating with the Soviet psychological tradition of Vygotsky, Luria, and Leont'ev in the 1920s, Activity Theory posits a three-level hierarchical analysis of motivated human action [cite: 1, 2]. The hierarchy consists of the overarching activity (driven by a macro-level motive), the constituent actions (driven by conscious, specific goals), and the underlying operations (automated, unconscious routines tailored to immediate environmental conditions) [cite: 1, 3]. A foundational principle of Activity Theory is the inherent fluidity and transience of these levels. When an operation proceeds smoothly, it requires negligible cognitive attention; however, when the environment introduces friction or an operation fails—a phenomenon termed a "breakdown"—the automated process is immediately elevated to the level of a conscious action requiring high-attention problem-solving [cite: 3, 4]. For example, the physical act of shifting gears in a vehicle remains an unconscious operation until the gearbox jams, at which point it forces cognitive engagement and becomes a deliberate action [cite: 3]. 

Engeström extended this foundational hierarchy to a macro-sociological level, mapping four levels of systemic contradictions (primary, secondary, tertiary, and quaternary) within human activity systems [cite: 4]. These contradictions serve as the engine for expansive learning and system evolution, demonstrating how systemic breakdowns reveal the underlying structural layers of the environment and the division of labor [cite: 4]. Similarly, Cognitive Systems Engineering and Ecological Interface Design (EID), pioneered by Rasmussen and Vicente, utilize the Abstraction Hierarchy and the Skills-Rules-Knowledge taxonomy [cite: 5, 6, 7]. The Skills-Rules-Knowledge taxonomy maps directly onto the concept of attention-cost rising with abstraction, demonstrating how cognitive control shifts from automated skill-based behavior to rule-based execution, and finally to knowledge-based problem solving when an operator faces unfamiliar or degraded environmental conditions [cite: 8, 9].

While Activity Theory and Ecological Interface Design fundamentally pre-empt the concept of an abstraction ladder mediated by systemic breakdowns, they are inextricably bound to human consciousness, socio-cultural tool-mediation, and biological cognitive load. The proposed framework diverges by stripping away the socio-cultural psychology and applying this ladder strictly to the structural-valence within frozen LLM policies. In this context, a "breakdown" does not trigger human psychological awareness, but rather forces a computational token-attention shift. This specific application to machine-state token economics represents a partial novelty, adapting a psychological construct into a purely mechanistic and structural instrument.

### Discrepancy Resolution and Affordance Solicitation as Co-Equal Grammars

The treatment of discrepancy-resolution and affordance-solicitation as two strictly independent, co-equal grammars is the most structurally contrarian and novel claim within Layer A. The contemporary theoretical landscape of cognitive science, predictive processing, and ecological psychology has aggressively moved to unify these phenomena, systematically subordinating one to the other under a single thermodynamic or informational imperative.

In the dominant paradigms of Active Inference and the Free Energy Principle championed by Friston, organisms maintain their structural and physiological integrity by minimizing variational free energy, which serves as an upper bound on sensory surprise or prediction error [cite: 10, 11]. This prediction error is functionally synonymous with the concept of discrepancy. Bruineberg and Rietveld explicitly merge the Gibsonian concept of affordances with the Free Energy Principle through the Skilled Intentionality Framework [cite: 12, 13, 14]. They define "skilled intentionality" as the tendency of an organism toward an "optimal grip" on a field of affordances [cite: 15, 16]. Crucially, Bruineberg and Rietveld define an affordance "solicitation" simply as a prediction error at the level of the brain-body-environment system [cite: 13, 17]. In their unified view, soliciting an affordance and resolving a discrepancy are the exact same thermodynamic operation: reducing systemic dis-attunement between the organism and its ecological niche [cite: 13]. 

Similarly, Pezzulo and Cisek explore the navigation of affordance landscapes through the lens of feedback control, arguing that multiple competing affordances are selected and enacted based on continuous, real-time discrepancy reduction [cite: 18, 19, 20]. In these prevailing models, affordances do not constitute an independent grammar; rather, they are merely the externalized, actionable pathways through which internal discrepancy (prediction error) is resolved to maintain biological homeostasis [cite: 21, 22, 23]. The entire field is predicated on the unification of these concepts because biological entities possess an inherent metabolic imperative to minimize entropy over time.

By declaring discrepancy and affordance as independent and co-equal, the proposed framework deliberately breaks from the biological necessity that drives the Free Energy Principle. A frozen, instruction-tuned LLM agent does not possess a biological metabolism, nor does it face the existential threat of thermodynamic dissolution if it fails to act. It does not possess internal drives that *require* the unification of affordance and discrepancy. Therefore, affordance (the uptake of an open, low-friction action surface simply because it is available) and discrepancy (the generative repair of a broken or incomplete text state) can exist as parallel, non-subordinated vectorial forces. This explicit architectural separation against the overwhelming unification consensus of modern cognitive science constitutes a highly defensible, novel theoretical residual.

### Reference-Inversion via the Amortized Prior

The third component of Layer A relies on "reference-inversion," where the comparator state against which a discrepancy is measured is inferred from static structure via a pre-trained prior, with no dynamic reward or value signal biasing the outcome. The nearest conceptual precedents for this mechanism lie at the intersection of Perceptual Control Theory and the diagnostic engineering models of the Realis Institute.

In classical Perceptual Control Theory, behavior is understood as the control of perception, driven by a comparator that continuously checks current sensory input against an internal reference state. However, in standard control theory and traditional reinforcement learning models, the reference state is invariably tied to a predefined goal, a dynamic reward function, or an externally mandated setpoint. 

The exact phrasing and mechanical definition of "Reference Inversion" appears prominently in Structural Orientation Theory developed by the Realis Institute [cite: 24, 25]. Structural Orientation Theory defines "Verification Reference Inversion" as a specific structural condition under which a verification operation, despite being executed in good faith and being procedurally complete, ratifies a corrupted state because the reference being consulted has inverted its position with the artifact being verified [cite: 24, 25]. In this pathological condition, the artifact's prior state becomes the operative reference, entirely bypassing any authoritative, upstream normative standard [cite: 24, 25]. The diagnostic fingerprint of this phenomenon is that each verification cycle merely ratifies the prior cycle, creating a self-referential loop devoid of external grounding [cite: 24]. Furthermore, in adaptive control theory, the concept of a "model-reference inverse" is utilized where an adaptive controller precedes a physical plant, utilizing a reference model to minimize output error without fundamentally altering the plant's internal dynamics [cite: 26].

While Structural Orientation Theory meticulously maps the exact structural pathology of reference-inversion in physical load-bearing systems and complex institutional architectures [cite: 25], the proposed framework performs a conceptual transposition, adapting this diagnostic pathology into a generative mechanism for language models. Because the LLM's policy is frozen through prior instruction tuning, the "reference state" against which discrepancies are measured is amortized directly into its neural weights. The agent does not seek an external reward; instead, it acts to collapse the environmental state into alignment with the grammatical and structural expectations embedded deeply within its prior. Next-token completion becomes the foundational base case of discrepancy resolution. This application of inverted control theory pathology to autoregressive generation is a novel instrumentalization, successfully stripping away the requirement for ongoing value-signal updates.

## Layer B: Environment as Geometry and Thermodynamics

Moving beyond the vector forces of direction and acceleration inherent to discrepancy and affordance, Layer B posits that the environment exerts non-vectorial geometric and thermodynamic constraints that filter, fold, stretch, and block the action space. This framework aggressively shifts away from the simplified, discrete states of standard Markov Decision Process grid worlds, proposing a physicalist understanding of computational spaces defined by five meta-dimensions: Horizon, Viscosity, Granularity, Temporality, and Plasticity.

The closest existing taxonomy to this "whole map" macroscopic approach resides at the intersection of Cognitive Work Analysis and Information Foraging Theory [cite: 5, 7, 27]. Cognitive Work Analysis, particularly through the lens of Ecological Interface Design, models the environment strictly as a set of physical and functional constraints rather than an expected sequence of tasks [cite: 8]. It treats the operational workspace as a complex ecology that bounds and shapes behavior irrespective of the agent's specific, localized goals. Concurrently, Information Foraging Theory, pioneered by Pirolli and Card, borrows heavily from behavioral ecology to model how human agents navigate abstract information spaces [cite: 27]. By utilizing concepts such as information scent, patch models, and diet selection, it inherently addresses environmental occlusion, the varying costs of interaction, and complex topological structures [cite: 27].

However, neither Cognitive Work Analysis nor Information Foraging Theory formalizes a strict five-dimensional, non-vectorial geometry in the precise manner proposed. The proposed framework is effectively a highly deliberate recombination of owned components curated from diverse fields into a single, unified analytical instrument tailored for POSIX environments. To avoid redundant theorization and establish rigorous academic lineage, it is necessary to identify the nearest named construct for each of the five meta-dimensions.

### I. Horizon & Occlusion ("Fog of War")

The first dimension describes the visibility boundary, where phenomena like hidden files, opaque binaries, and deep directory trees force exploratory action prior to target engagement. 

The nearest existing constructs are Partial Observability in reinforcement learning and the concept of a Markov Blanket in Active Inference. In computational models, occlusion is rigorously handled by Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes, where the agent cannot access the true underlying state of the environment and must instead maintain a probabilistic belief state over hidden variables. Within the biological and cognitive frameworks of Active Inference, the Markov Blanket strictly defines the statistical and physical boundary between the internal state of a system and the external environment [cite: 11]. It acts as the ultimate horizon of direct perception, mediating all sensory impressions and active states. Furthermore, Information Foraging Theory utilizes the concept of information patches, recognizing that an agent's view is occluded until they expend the energy to navigate to a new patch to assess its specific yield [cite: 27]. When articulating this dimension within the framework, citing Friston's formulation of Markov Blankets and Kaelbling's foundational work on Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes provides the most robust theoretical anchor.

### II. Viscosity & Friction (The Energy and Token Cost of Action)

The second dimension addresses the energy or token cost of acting within the environment—such as command latency, processing massive log files, or enduring slow compilations—which distorts trajectories, stalls progress, and induces the agent to seek computational shortcuts.

The nearest exact construct is "Viscosity," a term canonically defined and extensively explored in Thomas Green and Marian Petre's *Cognitive Dimensions of Notations* framework [cite: 28, 29, 30]. Developed to evaluate the usability of programming languages, visual interfaces, and notational systems, the framework defines viscosity precisely as the "resistance to change" within an information structure [cite: 30, 31]. A highly viscous system requires an undue number of user actions to accomplish a single goal [cite: 28]. Green and Petre further subdivide this into "repetition viscosity" (requiring many identical actions, analogous to paging through thousands of lines of terminal output) and "knock-on viscosity" (where a single change violates internal constraints, requiring cascading corrective actions, analogous to resolving nested dependency conflicts in a POSIX system) [cite: 28, 30]. Optimal Control Theory also touches upon this through the "cost of control," but the Cognitive Dimensions of Notations framework provides a phenomenological mapping that perfectly aligns with the concept of token-cost friction distorting an agent's intended path.

### III. Granularity of Action (The Quantization of Intervention)

The third dimension examines the level of abstraction at which the environment forces an intervention, contrasting low-level actions like assembly editing with high-level actions like API calls.

The nearest constructs are the Abstraction Hierarchy in Ecological Interface Design and Luciano Floridi's Method of Levels of Abstraction. Within Ecological Interface Design, the Abstraction Hierarchy explicitly models the granularity at which a complex sociotechnical system can be understood and manipulated, ranging from its fundamental physical form up to its generalized, abstract functions and ultimate purposes [cite: 5, 8]. In the philosophy of information, Floridi's Levels of Abstraction provides a rigorous formal epistemology demonstrating how the chosen granular constraints dictate the types of systemic interaction that are possible. In the domain of reinforcement learning, the Options framework attempts to model both temporal and action granularity via the creation of macro-actions. Vicente and Rasmussen's Abstraction Hierarchy serves as the most appropriate canonical citation for modeling the forced strata of intervention in complex workspaces.

### IV. Temporality & Rhythm (The Environment's Clock)

The fourth dimension acknowledges that the environment possesses its own internal clock and decay rate—manifesting as ephemeral states, background processes, or expiring access credentials—which imposes urgency and induces myopic planning.

The nearest constructs are Non-stationarity in reinforcement learning and Information Dynamics in complex systems. In machine learning, environments that evolve or degrade independently of the agent's specific actions are governed by the mathematics of non-stationarity, fundamentally complicating policy optimization. Furthermore, in fields ranging from music cognition to complex systems theory, Information Dynamics models the probabilistic structure of temporal sequences, dictating the underlying "rhythm" of expected states and the cognitive effort required to track them over time [cite: 32]. The concept of decaying environmental state maps cleanly onto discounting factors required to survive in non-stationary Markov Decision Processes.

### V. Plasticity & Hysteresis (Environmental Memory)

The final dimension questions whether the environment possesses memory. It explores how a prior action permanently reshapes the physics governing subsequent actions—such as editing a PATH variable, executing global software installs, or altering file permissions—whereby the agent alters the very field that simultaneously pushes and pulls it.

The nearest conceptual anchors are Niche Construction from evolutionary biology, Stigmergy from complex systems, and Hysteresis from physics. Niche Construction, formalized by Odling-Smee, perfectly captures the dynamic whereby an agent permanently reshapes its physical environment, which in turn drastically alters the selective pressures and affordances acting upon future generations or future actions [cite: 33]. Stigmergy, defined by Heylighen, explains indirect coordination through environmental modification; leaving a permanent structural change in a POSIX system (like a compiled binary or a modified configuration file) acts as a stigmergic signal to the agent's future self [cite: 34]. Additionally, Hysteresis describes physical and dynamic systems whose current state depends inextricably on their history, meaning that a prior action permanently alters the underlying attractor landscapes and phase space of the system, preventing reversible return paths [cite: 35, 36].

## The Dominant Pre-emptions and the Defensible Residual

The comprehensive analysis establishes that the proposed framework does not invent the foundational concepts of abstraction ladders, affordance landscapes, predictive discrepancies, or environmental viscosity from whole cloth. The abstraction ladder, rendered visible by operational breakdown, is powerfully pre-empted by Activity Theory and Ecological Interface Design. The concept of environmental viscosity resisting action is directly pre-empted by the Cognitive Dimensions of Notations. The environmental dimensions encompassing plasticity and visibility horizons are similarly pre-empted by evolutionary biology (Niche Construction), complex systems (Stigmergy), and reinforcement learning models (Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes). Reference-inversion, functioning without a dynamic reward signal, is structurally mapped by the Realis Institute's Structural Orientation Theory.

Given the density of these dominant pre-emptions, it is evident that the "whole map" is not a creation of fundamentally new scientific phenomena, but rather a sophisticated recombination of owned components. However, this recombination yields a highly robust and genuinely novel instrumentalization. 

The precise defensible residual lies in the application of these recombined biological, cognitive, and physicalist theories to the alien computational architecture of a frozen, instruction-tuned LLM agent. Traditional reinforcement learning agents and biological organisms adapt continuously; their internal states are fluid, and they face existential requirements to maintain equilibrium. Because of this dynamic, theories like Active Inference are forced by thermodynamic reality to unify affordance and discrepancy—an organism must resolve prediction errors through affordance uptake to survive. 

An LLM's weights, however, are frozen. It possesses an "amortized prior" encoded during pre-training, but it does not experience ongoing thermodynamic decay in its neural structure while operating in a POSIX environment. Therefore, the biological necessity to unify discrepancy and affordance completely vanishes. 

The framework's most powerful, publishable residual claim is the deliberate theoretical decoupling of discrepancy and affordance into independent, co-equal grammars, enacted against a reference-inverted prior, and situated within a non-vectorial geometric environment. No existing theory occupies this specific intersection because the entire canon of existing theory was architected for humans, biological organisms, or continuously updating reinforcement learning agents. By lifting these dimensional concepts out of biology and human-computer interaction and rigidly mapping them onto a POSIX workspace for a static language model, the framework provides an unprecedented and defensible instrument for structural-valence cartography in modern AI systems.
