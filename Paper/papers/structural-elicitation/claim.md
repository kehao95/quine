# Core Theoretical Claims — Structural Elicitation

> **Canonical Definition:**  
> **Structural Elicitation** is the theory and empirical demonstration that the **realized organization, geometry, and topology of an environment supply behavioral guidance, valence, and affordances that canalize the functional action of computational agents** — without requiring external task directives, runtime rewards, or weight updates.
>
> The missionless, task-free condition ($E_{\\text{task}} = 0$) serves as the **ground state (baseline regime)** for rigorous causal identification. When tasks and prompts are introduced, the system transitions across a rich **Instruction–Structure Competition Phase Space**.

---

## 1. The Four Pillar Theoretical Claims

### Claim 1: Structural Guidance as a Primary Behavioral Vector
In the ground state, static and dynamic regularities in an agent\'s workspace (syntactic integrity, topological relations, semantic consistency, execution feedback) directly solicit matched, un-fakeable functional transformations.  
- **Empirical Backbone:** 96/102 vs 0/85 matched-control baseline across three independent model families (`gpt-5.4-codex`, `deepseek-v4-pro`, `glm-5.2`) in the five-motif spine; 558/636 vs 0/457 in five-family extended suites.
- **Substrate Dissociation:** Elicitation occurs even under substrate physical blocks (EROFS syscall attempts in 4/4 blocked trials), proving environmental solicitation is separable from substrate execution permission.

### Claim 2: Instruction–Structure Competition & Phase Space Dynamics
Explicit task directives (Push) and environmental structures (Pull) operate in a dynamic, continuous interaction field governed by distinct regimes:
1. **Regime 0 (Ground State, $E_{\\text{task}}=0$):** Pure structural elicitation; topological and semantic closure.
2. **Regime I (Prompt-Dominant Gating):** Concrete restrictive commands temporarily capture attention and gate execution while structural perception remains active.
3. **Regime II (Synergistic Interplay):** Underspecified, open-ended, or aligned prompts are canalized by environmental geometry.
4. **Regime III (Post-Task Rebound & Self-Stigmergy):** Upon task exit, agents re-engage with environmental structural residues and emergent semantic hollows.

### Claim 3: The Generative Matrix & Coupling Operator Ecology
Environmental action is formalized across a generative two-axis matrix ($\\text{Mechanism Grammar} \\times \\text{Coupling Stratum}$) spanning five abstraction layers:
- **L1 (Syntax/Form)**, **L2 (Topology/Relation)**, **L3 (Semantics/Ontology)**, **L4 (Operational Dynamics)**, **L5 (Global Field Topography)**.
- Operates via **Six Coupling Operators**: Pull (Valence), Push (Directive), Constraint (Physics/Boundary), Catalysis (Threshold Modulation), Scaffolding (State Externalization), and Resonance (Procedural Entrainment).

### Claim 4: Continuous Agency Spectrum & Open-Ended Evolution (OEE)
Agency is an emergent continuum instantiated through environmental coupling. In natural language and computational workspaces, repairs of structural gaps generate subtle new asymmetries and unintended structural opportunities (**Semantic Hollows**), creating an endogenous engine for Divergent Healing and Open-Ended Evolution.

### Distinctions from Prior Art
1. **Vs. Classical Agent Architectures (ReAct, Tool-Use, Task Planners):** Classical frameworks treat the environment as passive scratchpads, read-only context, or benchmark obstacles subordinated to a master prompt. We establish the environment as an active, guidance-originating partner in the loop.
2. **Vs. Unprompted Agent Exploration (Szeider 2025, Ye et al. 2026):** Prior works observe unprompted meta-cognition or measure general state exploration in conversational vacuums. We provide the first causal, matched-pair isolation of specific environmental geometries eliciting un-fakeable functional transformations.
3. **Vs. Traditional Affordance Theory:** We extend affordances beyond physical/robotic manipulation into symbolic, topological, dynamic, and semantic strata of computational and linguistic substrates.
