# Deep Research Query: ALife Substrates And Process Ecologies For The Agentic ALIFE Workshop Paper

## Context

We are preparing a `4`-page ALIFE 2026 workshop short paper:

> From Agent Orchestration to Process Ecology: Quine as a POSIX-Native ALife Medium

Quine is a POSIX-native LLM-agent runtime. Instead of representing an agent as a
framework object, persona, or node in an orchestration graph, Quine realizes
each LLM agent as a native operating-system process. This makes PID/process
incarnation, stdio, files, permissions, signals, resource limits, `fork`,
`exec`, `exit`, volatile context, durable traces, and public control surfaces
part of the experimental substrate.

The paper's core claim is an observability claim, not a life claim:

> process-native realization turns lifecycle, mortality, inheritance,
> coordination, successor viability, and governance from orchestration metadata
> into substrate-level observables.

## Core Research Question

Which ALife, complex systems, digital organism, artificial chemistry, and
computational-media literatures provide the best citation anchors for arguing
that different computational substrates make different life-like variables
observable, manipulable, and comparable?

## Detailed Questions

1. What classic ALife sources define artificial life as study of life-like
   organization in alternative media, not merely biological simulation?
2. Which sources discuss computational substrates, artificial chemistries,
   digital organisms, process calculi, or computational media as experimental
   habitats where life-like organization can be probed?
3. Which sources distinguish substrate-provided operators from emergent or
   organization-dependent variables such as death, persistence, inheritance,
   reproduction, communication, and constraint?
4. Which sources help frame Quine as a process ecology rather than a simulated
   organism, a benchmark, or a multi-agent framework?
5. What vocabulary should the workshop paper borrow or avoid when saying "POSIX primitives act
   as environmental physics"?
6. What are the best compact citations for a `4`-page workshop paper with only
   `6`-`8` total references?

## Suggested Search Directions

- Artificial Life foundational definitions: Langton, Bedau, Taylor, Banzhaf,
  Rasmussen, Packard.
- Digital organism / digital evolution substrates: Tierra, Avida, artificial
  chemistries.
- ALife substrate and medium discussions: non-biological media, synthetic
  habitats, process organization, "life as it could be".
- Computational process/ecology analogs: actor systems, process calculi,
  operating-system process models, computational ecosystems, but only where
  they help the ALife argument.
- Existing literature on "hard" vs "soft" ALife, embodied/situated artificial
  life, ecological ALife, and environment-mediated organization.

## Relevance Filters

Prioritize:

- primary sources and highly cited surveys;
- sources that give terminology usable in a short ALife workshop paper;
- sources that support substrate observability and experimental separability;
- sources that help avoid overclaiming that Quine agents are alive.

De-emphasize:

- papers only about biological simulation without substrate theory;
- papers only about LLM agents or MAS engineering;
- generic OS/process documentation unless it supports a precise analogy;
- speculative AI life essays without primary-source value.

## What To Extract

Return:

1. A one-paragraph verdict on the best ALife framing for the workshop paper.
2. A compact source table with columns:
   - source
   - tradition
   - useful concept
   - exact workshop-paper use
   - caution / non-claim
3. A proposed `3`-sentence literature paragraph for the workshop paper's introduction.
4. A ranked list of `4`-`6` citation candidates, indicating which `1`-`2`
   should survive if references must be severely compressed.
5. Terms to use and terms to avoid.

## Manuscript Payoff

This query should sharpen:

- introduction framing;
- Table 1 "What Process-Native Realization Makes Observable";
- language for Quine as an ALife medium without claiming life or full
  autopoiesis.

## Red Lines

Do not conclude that Quine agents are alive, autopoietic, or open-ended
evolutionary systems. The needed contribution is substrate observability.
