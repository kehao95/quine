# Context

We are developing a theoretical framework with six dimensions for AI self-replication. One dimension (D6) concerns the motive for replication: whether the agent is explicitly instructed/programmed to replicate (motivated replication) vs. whether replication-like behavior emerges without an explicit replication goal (emergent/motive-free succession). We have pilot data suggesting that AI agents in an environment with no explicit replication instruction can still produce functional commons and lineage-like continuation through ecological selection pressure alone. We need to survey the theory frontier on emergent/motive-free replication and successor formation.

# Research questions

1. Has anyone studied EMERGENT self-replication in AI systems?
   - Replication-like behavior arising without an explicit replication goal/instruction
   - Spontaneous proliferation of AI agents without programmed self-copying
   - "Accidental" or "incidental" replication in agentic AI systems

2. What is the state of open-ended evolution (OEE) in AI/ML systems?
   - AI systems that produce ongoing novel complexity
   - Self-improving systems and their relationship to replication
   - Open-endedness in LLM agents, multi-agent systems, or code-generating systems
   - Any recent (2023-2025) OEE work specifically involving LLM-based agents

3. Has anyone studied successor formation WITHOUT explicit replication motive?
   - Agents that delegate, spawn, or transfer to new instances for task reasons (not replication goals)
   - Role succession: an agent taking over another's operational position without being a "copy"
   - Functional lineage continuation: work/obligations continuing across agent instances without explicit copying

4. What biological/computational theory motivates motive-free replication?
   - Is reproduction always motive-driven in biology? (No — but how is this formalized?)
   - Self-organization producing replication-like dynamics
   - Autocatalytic sets, self-maintaining systems
   - Maturana/Varela autopoiesis as it relates to AI

5. Has anyone reported AI agents producing replication-like behavior when:
   - The task is NOT "replicate yourself"
   - The agent has compute/resource access but no copy instruction
   - The agent is in a multi-agent environment with selection pressure
   - The agent has tools for spawning/delegating to other agents for task purposes

6. What are the safety implications discussed?
   - Unintended proliferation
   - Goal misspecification leading to replication-like behavior
   - Resource competition dynamics in agentic systems
   - Any discussion of "emergent replication" as an AI safety risk
