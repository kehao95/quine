## Context

Quine experiments show different successor forms under different resources and
post-handoff pressures: contract-level source-absent successor, reduced custom
stream successor, and rebuilt runtime successor with native control-surface
closure. A future paper route may frame this as computational or software
morphogenesis:

> process-native self-reproduction is a special case of pressure-conditioned
> computational morphogenesis: a lineage develops the successor morphology that
> can cross its lifecycle bottleneck under available resources and imposed
> post-handoff viability pressure.

This is not current LBA language. It needs novelty and metric validation before
it can become a paper claim.

Possible operational metrics:

- generated successor code structure
- exposed control surfaces
- fd / carrier topology across handoff
- syscall or process-interaction traces
- runtime-tool use after handoff
- return to a waiting state required for further addressability

## Core Research Question

Is `pressure-conditioned computational morphogenesis` of process successors a
publishable novel framing, or is it already covered by ALife morphogenesis,
morphogenetic engineering, artificial embryogeny, generative encodings,
software evolution, autonomic computing, or morphological computation?

More sharply: does prior work already model software/process successor bodies
as a morphospace shaped by resources and environmental/task pressure, with
metrics comparable to code structure, control surfaces, carrier topology, and
post-handoff behavior?

## Detailed Questions

1. **ALife morphogenesis:** What do reaction-diffusion, cellular automata,
   L-systems, morphogenetic engineering, and artificial embryogeny contribute?
   Are they about spatial form, developmental encoding, control, or successor
   reproduction?
2. **Computational morphospace:** Are there known morphospace frameworks for
   programs, digital organisms, neural architectures, robot bodies, software
   architectures, or agent bodies?
3. **Morphological computation:** Does embodied AI / soft robotics literature
   already frame morphology as task-solving computation? How close is that to
   Quine's reduced stream successor versus rebuilt runtime body?
4. **Software morphology:** Are there software-engineering or program-analysis
   traditions using morphology, architecture shape, AST metrics, call graphs,
   interface topology, or runtime interaction graphs in a comparable way?
5. **Developmental resources:** Do generative encodings and evo-devo ALife
   already explain `resource x pressure -> form` enough that Quine's framing is
   only an analogy?
6. **Successor morphogenesis:** Are there systems where the successor's body is
   not merely a phenotype but is constructed specifically to cross a lifecycle
   or task-continuity bottleneck?
7. **Metric design:** Which metrics would make Quine morphogenesis operational
   rather than metaphorical? Which are likely publishable and reproducible?
8. **Novelty stress test:** What is the strongest defensible claim and what
   should remain a future-work metaphor?

## Relevance Filters

Prioritize primary ALife, evo-devo, morphogenetic engineering, artificial
embryogeny, generative encoding, morphological computation, and software
architecture/program-analysis sources. Include recent surveys if they map the
field but trace claims to primary sources.

Include works that help distinguish:

- spatial biological morphogenesis versus computational/software morphology
- developmental encoding versus runtime successor construction
- phenotype morphospace versus process-successor morphospace
- morphology as task-solving body versus copying/reproduction semantics
- metric-backed claims versus metaphorical language

Exclude generic use of `morphology` as style unless it offers metrics or a
formal framework.

3. **Operational metric recommendations** for Quine successor morphologies.
4. **Possible paper theses:** conservative, medium, ambitious.

## Red Lines

Do not claim current Quine evidence already proves biological morphogenesis,
spatial morphogen fields, endogenous development, or a dense quantitative
morphospace. The goal is to test whether the future route is publishable and
what evidence would make it real.
